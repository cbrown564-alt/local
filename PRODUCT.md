# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary audience is independent owner-operated businesses in Dundrum and Newcastle, County Down. The next audiences, in order, are charities, clubs, public services, and larger chains. Visitors are busy local decision-makers who need to understand the value of better digital presentation quickly and without technical language.

## Product Purpose

The site shows local organisations what their public digital presence could become through personalised, side-by-side transformations. It exists to make a modern website, richer content, stronger imagery, and a better Maps presence feel exciting, practical, and attainable. Success begins when an owner requests a free before-and-after mock-up; the longer-term aim is affordable paid work supported by visible community contributions.

## Positioning

A local studio that invests in the digital appearance of its own community, showing each business a concrete transformation before asking it to buy.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: request a free before-and-after mock-up.
- Secondary CTA: browse local transformations.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: see what your local business could become online.
- Belief ladder: the studio understands this place; the current digital presence is underselling the real business; the proposed change is concrete and easy to judge; the work is affordable and low-risk; requesting a mock-up is worth doing now.
- Proof on hand: none yet. Initial work must be labelled clearly as concept transformations and must not imply client approval or a commercial relationship.

## Brand Personality

Vibrant, open, local. The first emotional response should be excitement about what a familiar business could become. The voice is neighbourly, direct, optimistic, and specific.

## Anti-references

The site must not resemble a generic AI or SaaS landing page. Avoid interchangeable technology claims, abstract automation imagery, dashboard theatre, inflated promises, and a hard-sell tone.

## Design Principles

- Make the transformation visible before explaining it.
- Use real local context and plain evidence instead of generic claims.
- Treat the existing business with respect: identify missed opportunities without mocking the owner or their current work.
- Make every next step feel small, clear, and reversible.
- Separate concept work, researched facts, and future client results truthfully.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal conformance target is claimed. Keep the core experience readable and usable with strong contrast, keyboard-operable controls, meaningful labels, and reduced-motion alternatives.
