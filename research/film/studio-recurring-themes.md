# Recurring themes — the faults owners recognise, staged as scenes

*Written 6 August 2026, for `research/studio-storytelling.md` §12 action 1
(PLAN.md §8). This is the stage that had to happen **before** more studio Omni
gens: the day-one slate in `studio-storytelling.md` §7 was formats in search of
a subject. These are the subjects.*

**What this is.** Twenty concepts have now been researched and redesigned. Each
carries three documented "changes" in `src/site/data/transformation-details.ts`
— roughly sixty observed faults on real public websites, each with the fix that
answered it. Read together, they repeat. Ten patterns account for nearly all of
them. Those patterns are the studio's real product story: not *we make nicer
websites*, but *here are ten specific things wrong with yours, and you already
know it.*

**What it is for.** Each theme becomes a self-contained scene — no business
named, no context needed, no voiceover required — that stages the fault and
then lands the fix as one physical gesture. Scenes are the unit the format
bakeoff (`studio-storytelling.md` Phase 1) should now be run against.

**What it is not.** Not concept elevation — no guest business voice here, and no
concept is identifiable in any scene. Not a claim of results. The faults are
generalised deliberately: an owner should recognise the pattern in their own
site, not recognise a neighbour being made an example of.

## Stage 3 — Shared visual grammar

Locked here because every scene below depends on it, and because three of these
decisions are Omni-failure decisions rather than taste.

**Object theatre — no people, no faces, no hands.** Everything is staged with
objects, light and small clay architecture. This is not a style preference: it
resolves the honesty problem (no invented owners, no likenesses of real
locals), it avoids Omni's weakest output (hands, faces, lip-sync), and it keeps
each scene generalised — an object can stand for every business of a shape,
a face cannot. Where a customer must be present, they are **boots, a bicycle, a
folded note, an umbrella** — arriving and, when the fault bites, leaving.

**No lettering anywhere.** Kent taught the house rule: Omni letters blank
fascias. Every prompt below forbids text and every edit list opens with the
blanking pass. It is also the honesty requirement — a legible fake brand in an
anti-pattern scene reads as a real business being mocked. Where a scene needs
a word, it is added later as live DOM type, not burned in.

**One physical turn.** Each scene is two beats: **the fault, felt** → **the fix,
as a single physical gesture**. No third beat, no montage. If the fix cannot be
one gesture, the theme is not ready to shoot.

**The fault is felt as a departure, the fix as an entry.** The strongest
material in the corpus is not the ugly page — it is the customer who turns
away. Stage the loss, not the aesthetics.

**Palette and register.** County Down coastal light: slate, sea green, warm
amber window (as `studio-storytelling.md` §5). Silence and stop-motion Foley
carry the fault; the fix brings one warm sound. Dry, specific, never sneering —
the villain is always the *page*, never the owner and never a real competitor.

**Reduced motion.** Every scene must read as a single still. Beat two's frame is
the poster; if the still doesn't carry the theme, the scene has failed.

### T1 — The login at the door

**Message.** A stranger should not need an account to find your front door.

**Why an owner recognises it.** Five of twenty had no website at all, so the
first thing a searching stranger meets is a consent dialog and a login form,
with the business's own sentence greyed out behind it. Owners think of their
Facebook page as their front door. This scene shows them that it is a door with
someone else's lock.

**Scene.** A small clay shopfront at dusk, warm and open, everything visibly
alive inside. Boots walk up to it. A pane of frosted glass drops from above and
seals the doorway; the warm interior goes grey behind it, and two blank paper
forms flutter up and press flat against the glass at eye height. The boots stop
and turn away. Then the pane tips forward and falls flat like a drawbridge, the
grey lifts, the amber comes back — and the boots walk in over it.

**Storyboard.**

| Beat | s | Frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.0–1.5 | Static wide, lit clay shopfront, dusk. Boots enter frame right, approaching. |
| 2 | 1.5–3.0 | Frosted pane slams down across the doorway. Interior desaturates to slate. |
| 3 | 3.0–4.5 | Two blank paper rectangles rise and stick to the glass. Boots halt. Hold. |
| 4 | 4.5–5.5 | Boots turn and walk out of frame. Empty street. Silence. **(the loss)** |
| 5 | 5.5–7.0 | Pane tips forward, falls flat as a ramp. Amber floods back. |
| 6 | 7.0–8.0 | Boots return, walk in over the fallen pane. Freeze for loop. |

**Prompt.**

```
Text-to-video, 8 seconds. Miniature clay / paper-craft set, stop-motion, no
people, no faces, no hands in frame. A small clay shopfront at dusk with a
warm amber lit interior. A pair of clay boots walks up to the door. A sheet of
frosted glass drops down and seals the doorway; the interior colour drains to
cold slate grey behind it. Two blank white paper rectangles rise and press flat
against the glass. The boots stop, turn, and walk away out of frame. The street
is empty and still. Then the glass pane tips forward and falls flat like a
drawbridge, the amber light floods back, and the boots return and walk in over
it.

No text, no lettering, no logos, no signage anywhere. Blank fascia stays blank.
County Down coastal palette: slate, sea green, warm amber. Dry and calm, not
corporate explainer energy.

Audio: stop-motion Foley. A hard glass thud on the seal, then silence over the
departure, then one warm low chord as the pane falls open.
```

**Edit turns.** 1. `Keep everything. Remove all lettering and logos. Blank
panels and paper stay blank.` 2. `Hold the empty street longer — the departure
is the point.` 3. `Freeze the final half-second for a seamless loop.`

**Acceptance.** A viewer with no sound and no copy can say what went wrong.

### T4 — Nobody answers "are you open?"

**Message.** People arrive with one question. The first screen should answer it.

**Why an owner recognises it.** Five concepts buried the arrival question —
under a translation bar and a video, under an archive photograph and seven
uppercase menu items, inside a chat popup, or below the first scroll entirely.
Owners know their hours are on the site. They have never watched somebody fail
to find them.

**Scene.** Rain, dusk. A clay shopfront with a dark, unreadable window. An
umbrella approaches, hesitates in front of the glass, tilts as if reading, and
withdraws. The window stays dark. Then a small brass-rimmed clock face lights
in the window with one lamp behind it, and the amber reaches the wet pavement —
and the umbrella comes back and folds down at the door.

**Storyboard.**

| Beat | s | Frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.0–1.5 | Wide, rain, wet slate pavement, clay shopfront, window dark. |
| 2 | 1.5–3.5 | Umbrella enters, stops at the glass, tilts left then right. Nothing lights. |
| 3 | 3.5–5.0 | Umbrella withdraws, out of frame. Rain on empty pavement. **(the loss)** |
| 4 | 5.0–6.5 | A lamp warms behind the glass; a brass clock face resolves in the window. |
| 5 | 6.5–8.0 | Amber spills onto wet pavement; umbrella returns and folds down. Freeze. |

**Prompt.**

```
Text-to-video, 8 seconds. Miniature clay / paper-craft set, stop-motion, no
people, no faces, no hands. Rain at dusk on a wet slate pavement in front of a
small clay shopfront. Its window is completely dark and shows nothing. A small
clay umbrella moves up to the glass, pauses, tilts as if trying to read
something, then withdraws and leaves the frame. Rain falls on the empty
pavement. Then a warm lamp lights behind the window and a plain brass-rimmed
clock face becomes visible in it; warm amber light spreads across the wet
pavement. The umbrella returns and folds down at the door.

No text, no lettering, no numbers, no logos, no signage. The clock face has
hands but no numerals. County Down palette: slate, sea green, warm amber.

Audio: rain and stop-motion Foley. Rain alone over the departure. One soft
lamp-click and a warm hum as the window lights.
```

**Edit turns.** 1. `Remove all lettering and numerals. The clock has hands
only.` 2. `Keep the frame identical between the dark window and the lit window
— only light changes.` 3. `Slower on the hesitation.`

**Acceptance.** The dark-window frame and the lit-window frame are the same
composition — so a still pair works as a before/after on a page.

### T6 — Your worries, in front of their question

**Message.** Your website greets people with what worries you. They came with a
question.

**Why an owner recognises it.** Five concepts put the business's own anxieties
first: a newsletter capture above the farm, a 28-language translation widget as
the most prominent control, cancellation warnings before the booking widget,
controls wired to nothing — and the mirror image, an £850 original whose
collection-only terms sat in capitals *inside* the description, under a button
identical to a £3 card's. Every owner has added one of these on somebody's
advice.

**Scene.** A clay counter. On it, at the far end, the thing the customer came
for: a warm loaf under a small lamp. Boots arrive. Three stiff blank clipboards
swing up in sequence between the boots and the loaf, each one blocking a little
more, until the loaf is out of sight. Then the clipboards fold down flat and
slide sideways; two of them go under the counter, and the third — now a single
small folded card — is set down *beside* the loaf, not in front of it.

**Storyboard.**

| Beat | s | Frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.0–1.0 | Low side-on shot along a clay counter. Lit loaf at the far end. |
| 2 | 1.0–2.0 | Boots arrive at the near end. |
| 3 | 2.0–4.0 | Clipboard one swings up. Then two. Then three. The loaf disappears. |
| 4 | 4.0–5.0 | Hold. Nothing but blank boards. **(the loss)** |
| 5 | 5.0–6.5 | Boards fold flat; two slide away under the counter. |
| 6 | 6.5–8.0 | One small folded card settles beside the loaf. Lamp reaches both. Freeze. |

**Prompt.**

```
Text-to-video, 8 seconds. Miniature clay / paper-craft set, stop-motion, no
people, no faces, no hands. A low side-on view along a small clay shop counter.
At the far end, a warm loaf of bread sits under a small lamp. A pair of clay
boots arrives at the near end. Three stiff blank clipboards swing up one after
another between the boots and the loaf, each blocking more of it, until the
loaf cannot be seen at all. Hold still. Then the clipboards fold down flat and
slide sideways under the counter, leaving one small folded card, which settles
on the counter beside the loaf rather than in front of it. The lamp light
reaches both.

No text, no lettering, no forms, no logos — every board and card is blank
paper. County Down palette. Dry humour, gentle, never sneering.

Audio: stop-motion Foley, three sharp clipboard clacks, then a soft paper
settle.
```

**Edit turns.** 1. `Every clipboard and card stays completely blank — no
lettering, no ruled form fields.` 2. `The boards should feel bureaucratic and
slightly absurd, not threatening.` 3. `Freeze the final half-second.`

**Acceptance.** Funny without a caption; the loaf is never mocked.

### T7 — The best thing about you is in the footer

**Message.** The thing you'd say in ten seconds at the counter isn't on the site
at all.

**Why an owner recognises it.** The most common fault in the corpus, at six
concepts: fifty-three years and thirteen named projects with no screen showing
the record; five accreditation marks as small footer logos; a decade of
professional-kitchen recipes lost behind default navigation; a Great British
Menu chef existing only as feed fragments; fifty summers compressed into one
line; a café whose name means *twins*, for its twin founders, never explained.
This is the recognition theme — the studio's whole method in one scene.

**Scene.** A clay shopfront in ordinary daylight, unremarkable, correct. The
camera drops below the pavement to a dim cellar drawer under the shop, where a
brass object sits glowing faintly under dust — a medal, a worn tool, a bound
stack of dated paper. Then the drawer rises through the floor, and the object is
set on the inside of the windowsill, where street light catches it. The shopfront
is otherwise unchanged, and now unmistakable.

**Storyboard.**

| Beat | s | Frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.0–1.5 | Static wide, clay shopfront, flat daylight. Competent, forgettable. |
| 2 | 1.5–3.5 | Camera tilts and pushes down through the pavement into a dim cellar. |
| 3 | 3.5–4.5 | A dusty drawer; inside, a brass object with a faint glow. Hold. **(the loss)** |
| 4 | 4.5–6.0 | The drawer lifts; the object rises with it through the floor. |
| 5 | 6.0–8.0 | Same opening wide shot — object now on the windowsill, catching light. Freeze. |

**Prompt.**

```
Text-to-video, 8 seconds. Miniature clay / paper-craft set, stop-motion, no
people, no faces, no hands. Start on a static wide shot of a small, plain,
competent clay shopfront in flat daylight. The camera tilts down and pushes
through the pavement into a dim cellar beneath the shop, revealing a dusty
wooden drawer holding a single brass object — a small medal or worn hand tool —
with a faint warm glow on it. Hold. Then the drawer lifts and carries the
object up through the floor, and it comes to rest on the inside of the shop's
windowsill where daylight catches the brass. Return to exactly the opening wide
shot: the same shopfront, unchanged, with the brass object now visible glinting
in the window.

No text, no lettering, no logos, no signage. Blank fascia stays blank. County
Down palette: slate, sea green, warm brass. Reverent, not whimsical.

Audio: stop-motion Foley, a drawer scrape, a single soft brass chime on the
final frame.
```

**Edit turns.** 1. `Remove all lettering and engraving from the brass object.`
2. `The final shot must match the first shot's framing exactly.` 3. `Less
whimsy — this beat is quietly serious.`

**Acceptance.** First and last frames are the same composition. That pair *is*
the studio's before/after argument, and it is the T7 poster.

### T2 — The dead end

**Message.** Every link that ends in nothing was somebody deciding to visit you.

**Why an owner recognises it.** Six concepts: a domain that no longer resolves;
a domain redirecting over plain HTTP to a parent company's page under another
name; a shop whose search results are crowded by same-named shops in another
country; and a gallery where 124 of 232 listed originals had sold, so arriving
on one from a search or a saved link was a full stop. Owners think of a dead
link as a technical problem. It is a person who wanted to come.

**Scene.** A folded paper note flies along a clay street, steady and purposeful,
aimed at a door — and passes clean through the gap where the door should be,
into an empty gravel lot, where it drops and lies flat. Stillness. Then the same
flight again: the note arrives, and this time a door is there. It slots into the
letterbox, and a lamp comes on above.

**Storyboard.**

| Beat | s | Frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.0–2.0 | Tracking with a folded paper note flying low along a clay street. |
| 2 | 2.0–3.0 | Ahead: a gap between two buildings, empty gravel lot behind it. |
| 3 | 3.0–4.5 | Note passes through, drops, lies flat on the gravel. Hold. **(the loss)** |
| 4 | 4.5–6.0 | Cut back — same flight, same street, same speed. |
| 5 | 6.0–8.0 | A door is there. Note slots into the letterbox; a lamp lights above it. Freeze. |

**Prompt.**

```
Text-to-video, 8 seconds. Miniature clay / paper-craft street, stop-motion, no
people, no faces, no hands. A small folded paper note flies low and steadily
along a narrow clay street, as if delivering itself. Ahead of it, between two
buildings, is a gap — no door, no building, just an empty gravel lot. The note
flies through the gap, drops, and lies flat on the gravel. Hold on the still
empty lot. Then repeat the same flight along the same street at the same speed,
but now a clay door with a brass letterbox stands in the gap. The note slots
into the letterbox and a small lamp lights above the door.

No text, no lettering, no addresses, no logos, no signage. County Down palette:
slate, sea green, warm amber, brass. Melancholy on the first pass, warm on the
second.

Audio: stop-motion Foley and paper flutter. Wind and silence over the empty
lot. A brass letterbox snap and a lamp click at the end.
```

**Edit turns.** 1. `Remove all lettering — the note and the door are blank.`
2. `Both flights must follow the identical path and speed.` 3. `Longer, emptier
hold on the gravel.`

**Acceptance.** The two flights are identical enough that only the ending
differs. Also cuts as a 404-page loop — see `omni-clip-backlog.md` A30.

### T3 — Whose shop is this?

**Message.** The biggest name on your website should be yours.

**Why an owner recognises it.** Four concepts handed their opening screen to
somebody else: a full-width supplier wordmark with the shop's own name only in
the small header logo; a wall of stock imagery while the practice's real badge
sat small in a corner; an opening on the generic destination rather than the
building people arrive at; a public route branded as a parent company. Owners
were usually *given* those assets and told they added credibility.

**Scene.** A clay shopfront almost completely obscured by oversized glossy
blank shields — abstract crest shapes in loud plastic colours — propped across
the fascia and leaning in the window. Small, on the doorpost, a hand-cut brass
mark, barely visible. Then the shields shrink and settle into a modest neat row
along the sill, and the brass mark grows and takes the fascia.

**Storyboard.**

| Beat | s | Frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.0–2.0 | Wide. Shopfront lost behind five or six oversized blank glossy shields. |
| 2 | 2.0–3.5 | Slow push in; the small brass doorpost mark is found. Hold. **(the loss)** |
| 3 | 3.5–5.5 | Shields shrink in place, lower, and line up along the windowsill. |
| 4 | 5.5–8.0 | Brass mark rises and enlarges onto the empty fascia. Freeze. |

**Prompt.**

```
Text-to-video, 8 seconds. Miniature clay / paper-craft set, stop-motion, no
people, no faces, no hands. A small clay shopfront is almost entirely hidden
behind five or six oversized glossy blank shields — plain abstract crest and
badge shapes in loud plastic colours, completely without markings — propped
across the fascia and leaning in the window. The camera pushes in slowly and
finds a small hand-cut brass emblem on the doorpost, barely visible. Then the
glossy shields shrink where they stand, lower themselves, and line up in a
modest neat row along the windowsill. The small brass emblem rises and enlarges
onto the now-empty fascia above the door.

No text, no lettering, no logos, no recognisable brand marks — every shield and
emblem is a plain blank shape. County Down palette against deliberately
clashing plastic colours for the shields.

Audio: stop-motion Foley. Plastic clatter as the shields shrink; one warm
metallic settle as the brass mark seats.
```

**Edit turns.** 1. `Every shield must be entirely blank — no lettering, no
marks, nothing resembling a real brand.` 2. `The shields end up tidy and
respectful, not thrown away.` 3. `Push-in slower.`

**Acceptance.** No frame contains anything a viewer could mistake for a real
supplier's mark. The shields are *demoted, not destroyed* — the concepts kept
the suppliers, in one confident line.

### T5 — The bell with no clapper

**Message.** "Get in touch" means the customer does the work.

**Why an owner recognises it.** A wedding venue with no enquiry form at all, so
every enquiry had to become a phone call first; a "Book Appointments" link
opening a page that listed only a phone number and an email; a workshop page
that explained repairs and then said contact the shop. In each case the action
existed as a *word*, not as a mechanism.

**Scene.** A brass counter bell. Boots arrive; the bell is struck — a dead
wooden thud, no ring. Struck again. Nothing. Behind, in a dim back room, a
telephone rings on and on in an empty chair's company. The boots leave. Then the
bell is struck once and rings clear; a folded card drops through a slot into a
tray, and the lamp in the back room comes on.

**Storyboard.**

| Beat | s | Frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.0–1.0 | Close on a brass counter bell. Boots arrive at frame edge. |
| 2 | 1.0–2.5 | Bell struck: dull thud. Struck again: nothing. |
| 3 | 2.5–4.5 | Rack focus to a dim back room where a telephone rings, unanswered. |
| 4 | 4.5–5.5 | Boots leave. Bell alone. **(the loss)** |
| 5 | 5.5–7.0 | Bell struck once: clear ring. A folded blank card drops into a tray. |
| 6 | 7.0–8.0 | Back-room lamp lights. Freeze. |

**Prompt.**

```
Text-to-video, 8 seconds. Miniature clay / paper-craft set, stop-motion, no
people, no faces, no hands. Close on a small brass counter bell on a clay
counter. A pair of clay boots arrives at the edge of frame. The bell is pressed
down and makes only a dead wooden thud — no ring. It is pressed again: nothing.
Focus shifts to a dim back room behind the counter where an old telephone is
ringing, unanswered, beside an empty chair. The boots turn and leave. The bell
sits alone. Then the bell is pressed once and rings clear and bright, a small
folded blank card drops through a slot into a tray on the counter, and a lamp
comes on in the back room.

No text, no lettering, no logos, no signage. The card is blank paper. County
Down palette: slate, brass, warm amber.

Audio: stop-motion Foley. Two dead thuds. A telephone ringing unanswered under
silence. Then one clear bell, a paper drop, a lamp click.
```

**Edit turns.** 1. `The bell must be pressed by nothing visible — no hands in
frame.` 2. `Card stays blank, no lettering.` 3. `Let the unanswered telephone
run longer.`

**Acceptance.** The dead thud is audible and the ring is a relief. This is the
one scene where audio carries the theme — sidecar must note whether the mute
still works, or whether T5 is a sound-on-only piece.

### T8 — Locked in a file

**Message.** A menu nobody opens is a menu nobody read.

**Why an owner recognises it.** Five menus as PDF downloads, so no dish could be
read without opening a file. A brochure as a dated PDF. A full delivery
schedule published as dense paragraphs of town names, so a customer had to read
the lot to find their own town. A working set of filters behind a menu that
asked which of ninety-two makers you wanted first. The information was there
every time. The shape was wrong every time.

**Scene.** A stiff sealed envelope nailed flat to a shop door. Boots arrive; the
envelope is tugged at the corner and doesn't give. Again — nothing. The boots
leave. Then the envelope's seal releases and its contents unfold down the door
as a long concertina strip, panel after panel, legible from the street: ruled
bands and small shapes, no words, one panel clearly the day you want.

**Storyboard.**

| Beat | s | Frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.0–1.5 | Front-on, clay shop door. A stiff sealed envelope nailed to it. |
| 2 | 1.5–3.0 | The corner lifts and drops back. Twice. It will not open. |
| 3 | 3.0–4.0 | Boots leave frame. Envelope hangs there. **(the loss)** |
| 4 | 4.0–7.0 | Seal releases; a concertina strip unfolds down the door, panel by panel. |
| 5 | 7.0–8.0 | Full strip visible, one panel warm-lit. Freeze. |

**Prompt.**

```
Text-to-video, 8 seconds. Miniature clay / paper-craft set, stop-motion, no
people, no faces, no hands. Front-on view of a small clay shop door with a
stiff, sealed, blank paper envelope nailed flat to it. A pair of clay boots
arrives; the envelope's corner lifts slightly and falls back, twice, refusing
to open. The boots leave. Then the seal releases and the envelope's contents
unfold downward across the door as a long concertina paper strip, panel after
panel, until the whole strip hangs readable from the street. Each panel carries
only ruled bands and small abstract shapes. One panel is lit warmer than the
rest.

No text, no lettering, no numbers, no logos. Ruled lines and blank shapes only.
County Down palette: slate, cream paper, warm amber.

Audio: stop-motion Foley. Two dry stiff-paper refusals, then a long satisfying
paper unfolding.
```

**Edit turns.** 1. `Panels carry ruled lines and blank shapes only — no
lettering or numerals anywhere.` 2. `Make the unfold longer and more
satisfying; that is the payoff.` 3. `Freeze the final half-second.`

**Acceptance.** The unfold is physically pleasurable to watch. If it isn't, the
scene has no reason to exist — the whole theme is *shape*.

### T9 — The stopped clock

**Message.** Your site is telling people how old it is, not how good you are.

**Why an owner recognises it.** One venue's copyright read 2018, its
testimonials ran 2017 to 2019 and its brochure was dated 2021.22 — beside a
menu uploaded that month. A maker's navigation still read *Home Page* and *Our
Store*. An attractions board carried three rows reading "Seasonal — check
Facebook". The business is alive; the page is not, and the two sit side by side.

**Scene.** Interior. On the wall, a clay clock, hands frozen, thick with dust,
cobweb across its face. On the counter below, in the same frame, fresh bread
steaming — the business plainly thriving. Then the dead clock lifts off the wall
and a small open aperture is behind it, through which real movement is visible:
steam, a turning page, a moving shadow. The dust is gone; the frame now holds
two live things.

**Storyboard.**

| Beat | s | Frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.0–2.0 | Interior wide: dusty stopped clock above, steaming fresh loaf below. |
| 2 | 2.0–3.5 | Push in on the clock. Dust, cobweb, motionless hands. Hold. **(the loss)** |
| 3 | 3.5–5.0 | The clock lifts away off the wall and out of frame. |
| 4 | 5.0–8.0 | Behind it, an open aperture showing live movement — steam, a turning page. Freeze. |

**Prompt.**

```
Text-to-video, 8 seconds. Miniature clay / paper-craft interior, stop-motion,
no people, no faces, no hands. A clay wall clock hangs above a small counter.
Its hands are motionless and it is thick with grey dust and a cobweb. On the
counter directly below, in the same frame, a fresh loaf of bread steams gently
— the business is clearly alive. The camera pushes in on the dead dusty clock
and holds. Then the clock lifts off the wall and out of frame, revealing a
small open aperture behind it through which live movement is visible: rising
steam and a page turning in the light beyond. No dust remains.

No text, no lettering, no numerals, no dates, no logos. The clock face has
hands only. County Down palette: slate, cream, warm amber.

Audio: stop-motion Foley. A dry dusty silence on the clock, then a soft draught
and a page turn.
```

**Edit turns.** 1. `No numerals, dates or lettering on the clock face.` 2. `The
steaming loaf must stay in frame throughout — the contrast is the point.`
3. `Dust needs to read clearly; add more.`

**Acceptance.** Both the dead thing and the live thing are in the same frame at
0.0s. This is the scene that argues for the honest handoff (let the fresh
source stay fresh; let the page hold what doesn't go stale) without a word of
jargon — see the feed test in `docs/the-elevation-method.md`.

### T10 — The swap test

**Message.** Swap your name for a competitor's. If nothing else needs changing,
the site isn't yours.

**Why an owner recognises it.** The studio's own test, and the corpus's
sharpest indictment: an arcade whose three listed attractions were the three
every seaside arcade would list; a bike shop where an archived review found
"nothing on the page is bike-shop-shaped"; the generic chassis named as a
failure mode in the method doc. This is the closer — the only theme that flatters
the owner rather than the fix, because the answer is *you are not generic; your
website is.*

**Scene.** Two identical clay shopfronts side by side. A small blank brass
nameplate is lifted from the first and pressed onto the second. It seats
perfectly. Nothing else changes. Hold — the joke lands as an accusation. Then
the second shopfront grows three small true things: a local hill silhouette
etched in the glass, a worn hollow in the doorstep, an odd brass door number
(no digits — just the shape of one). The plate is lifted again and now it will
not seat: it hovers, tilts, and won't go on.

**Storyboard.**

| Beat | s | Frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.0–1.5 | Static wide. Two identical clay shopfronts, symmetrical, flat light. |
| 2 | 1.5–3.0 | A blank brass plate lifts from shop one, crosses, seats on shop two. Perfect fit. |
| 3 | 3.0–4.0 | Hold on the perfect fit. Nothing has changed. **(the loss)** |
| 4 | 4.0–6.0 | Shop two grows three details: hill silhouette in the glass, worn step, brass number. |
| 5 | 6.0–8.0 | The plate lifts again, hovers, tilts, will not seat. Freeze mid-air. |

**Prompt.**

```
Text-to-video, 8 seconds. Miniature clay / paper-craft set, stop-motion, no
people, no faces, no hands. Static wide shot of two identical small clay
shopfronts side by side under flat even light. A small blank brass nameplate
lifts off the first shopfront, floats across, and seats itself perfectly on the
second. Nothing else about either shop changes. Hold on the perfect fit. Then
the second shopfront gains three small specific details: the silhouette of a
mountain ridge etched into its window glass, a worn hollow in its stone
doorstep, and a small blank brass door number plate. The nameplate lifts off
again, hovers in front of the second shop, tilts, and cannot seat itself. It
stays floating.

No text, no lettering, no numerals, no logos — every plate is blank brass.
County Down palette: slate, sea green, brass. Deadpan.

Audio: stop-motion Foley. A clean metallic click when the plate seats; a
scraping refusal when it will not.
```

**Edit turns.** 1. `All plates completely blank — no lettering or numerals.`
2. `Both shopfronts must be exactly identical in beat one, including light.`
3. `End on the plate still hovering — do not resolve it.`

**Acceptance.** Beat one is genuinely identical, and beat five genuinely
refuses. If the plate lands anyway, the scene argues the opposite of the point.
Pairs with the claymation `recognition` / `theatre` beats already specced as
A19 / S1.

## Stage 5 — Refinement pass

What the exploration produced and this pass cut, so the next person does not
re-propose it.

**Cut: the villain website.** An earlier line-up had a scene of purple-gradient
template sludge (the `studio-storytelling.md` §7 gen 3 idea, S6). Cut as a
*scene*, kept only as an optional insert. Reason: any legible fake site reads
as a real business being mocked, and the corpus's faults are not aesthetic —
none of the sixty is "it looked bad". Staging ugliness argues for prettiness,
which the method explicitly disowns. **"Anything that is merely nicer is not
elevation"** has to be true of the marketing too.

**Cut: the sad owner.** Several first-pass scenes had a clay owner waiting
behind a counter while customers failed to arrive. Cut under working agreement
7 (humour punches up or sideways). Sympathetic or not, it makes the owner the
subject of the fault. Object theatre fixed this: the *page* is the antagonist
in all ten scenes, and the owner is never on screen.

**Cut: numbers on screen.** Early drafts had counts as props — 124 sold, 28
languages, 92 makers, 53 years. All removed. They are real figures about real
named businesses, and once generalised into a scene they become claims the
studio cannot attach to anyone. The scenes carry *shape*, not data. Numbers, if
ever wanted, go on the page as live type beside the clip with the concept
named — never burned into a generalised frame.

**Cut: from ten themes to ten scenes, not twelve.** Dead domain and sold-out
were separate themes until it was clear a visitor cannot tell them apart — both
are a journey that ends — so they became one flight in T2. Insider taxonomy
(the 92-maker menu, the four-clicks-to-one-building navigation) was its own
theme until the fix turned out to be identical to the PDF fix: put it in the
shape the customer needs. It joined T8.

**Kept against instinct: T9's stopped clock.** Thinnest evidence at three
concepts, and the obvious cut. Kept because it is the theme owners volunteer
about themselves — everyone knows their site says an old year — and because it
is the only scene that argues for the honest handoff rather than for more
content. It earns its place by what it teaches, not by its tally.

**Sequenced, not just listed.** The ten cut in this order as a reel: T1 (you
can't get in) → T4 (you can't find out) → T5 (you can't ask) → T6 (you're
stopped at the door) → T8 (you can't read it) → T2 (you arrive nowhere) → T3
(it isn't yours) → T9 (it's out of date) → T7 (the good thing is hidden) →
T10 (so it could be anyone's). Faults of *access* first, faults of *identity*
second, and the swap test last, because T10 only lands once the audience has
watched nine specific things go wrong.
