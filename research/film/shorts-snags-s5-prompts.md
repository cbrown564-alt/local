## Fixed visual contract

Miniature clay and paper-craft set, stop-motion animation look, shot slightly
above eye level on a long lens with shallow depth of field. No people, no
faces, no hands, no lettering, no numerals, no logos, no signage, no readable
marks, no interface, no watermark. Calm and dry, not corporate explainer
energy. 16:9.

Keep the County Down coastal object-theatre world: slate roofs, deep
sea-green painted clay frontage, blue dusk, wet charcoal cobbles, restrained
warm amber window light and harbour brass. The blank nameplate, mounting marks
and paired shopfronts should stay consistent with
`media/film/stills/trailer/15-nameplate-lifting.png`,
`media/film/stills/trailer/16-nameplate-seated.png` and the T10 references
`media/film/stills/studio/T10-01-plate-left.png` and
`T10-02-plate-seated-right.png`. The distinctive object in shot 08/09 is a
folded paper note with a unique hand-pressed fold pattern, not writing.

## Continuity-test prompts

### 01 — bare fascia

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: research still for a narrated short.
Primary request: one small generic clay shopfront at blue dusk, square-on and
calm, with a deep sea-green painted frontage, dark slate roof, wet charcoal
cobbles and a warm amber glow behind the window. The fascia board is completely
bare except for two faint clean mounting marks where a small nameplate could
have been fixed; no plate is present. Leave the window plain and empty. Static
16:9 establishing composition, slightly above eye level, long lens and shallow
depth of field.
Style/medium: miniature clay and paper-craft set, stop-motion animation look.
Lighting/mood: blue coastal dusk, honest and slightly uncomfortable, never
punitive or mocking.
Color palette: slate, sea green, wet charcoal, restrained warm amber and
harbour brass only in tiny hardware details.
Materials: hand-shaped clay, paper-craft frontage, damp cobbles, softly
imperfect painted surfaces.
Constraints: no people, faces, hands, lettering, numerals, logos, signage,
readable marks, interface or watermark; no nameplate, no extra shopfront.

### 03 — lifted blank nameplate

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset type: research still for a narrated short.
Input images: trailer/15-nameplate-lifting.png and studio/T10-01-plate-left.png
as visual references for object identity, palette and shopfront construction;
do not copy their exact crop.
Primary request: the same single deep sea-green clay shopfront as shot 01,
seen square-on at blue dusk. A small plain blank harbour-brass nameplate has
just lifted away from the fascia and hangs mid-air a few inches above and to
the right of its two clean mounting marks, tilted slightly, as if caught in a
swap test. The plate is completely blank and unreadable. Keep the empty window,
dark slate roof, wet cobbles and small warm amber glow consistent with shot 01.
Static 16:9 act-break composition, slightly above eye level, long lens and
shallow depth of field.
Style/medium: miniature clay and paper-craft set, stop-motion animation look.
Lighting/mood: quiet sting, restrained and fair, no drama.
Color palette: slate, sea green, wet charcoal, warm amber and muted harbour
brass.
Materials: hand-shaped clay, worn blank brass, paper-craft frontage, damp
cobbles.
Constraints: no people, faces, hands, lettering, numerals, logos, signage,
readable marks, interface or watermark; no second shopfront; no engraved plate.

## Remaining prompts

02. Match shot 01 exactly, but place one plain unmarked paper card centred in
the window beneath the bare fascia. It should feel generic and competent, not
like an interface or a poster.

04. Match shot 02 exactly after the nameplate has been removed: the same paper
card, same window, same crop and same fascia mounting marks. Nothing else in
the frame changes.

05. Place the same blank brass nameplate down in the far corner of the window
sill, visibly marginal rather than mounted. Keep the fascia, paper card and
shopfront unchanged.

06. Wide, tidy, respectable single shopfront at dusk: clean slate roof,
sea-green frontage, warm window, orderly sill and cobbles. Make it the most
finished-looking frame in the short while keeping it generic and unmarked.

07. Pull back from shot 06 to reveal two neighbouring shopfronts. All three
must share the same roof, frontage, window arrangement and warm glow, reading
as near-identical silhouettes with no signs or names.

08. Return to the original shopfront and bare fascia mounting marks. Set one
distinctive folded paper note into the fascia position: an unusual pressed
fold pattern and handmade edge, but no writing, symbol or readable mark. It is
the one object that could not honestly belong to a competitor.

09. Close on the distinctive folded paper note held upright beside the same
blank brass nameplate, both isolated against the shopfront’s slate and
sea-green background. The paper’s fold pattern is physical and unique, never
lettering. The two objects should clearly not be interchangeable.
