# Ten postcards, not one leaflet

*Written 6 August 2026. Print outreach built on the fault taxonomy in
[`research/film/studio-recurring-themes.md`](film/studio-recurring-themes.md),
selected from the `faults` pass added to
[`docs/RESEARCH_METHOD.md`](../docs/RESEARCH_METHOD.md). Owned by the studio
storytelling workstream (`research/studio-storytelling.md` §3, in scope:
"outreach sheets").*

## The argument

A leaflet has to be true of every business it reaches, so it ends up saying
*we make nice websites for local businesses* — which is what every leaflet
says, and which fails the studio's own swap test. Ten postcards let the **first
line be about the recipient**, because by the time a postcard is addressed, the
research record already knows which of the ten faults their public surface
shows and when it was seen.

The cost is print setup for ten designs instead of one. The gain is that the
card stops being advertising and becomes a small piece of evidence.

**The selection rule is the whole idea:** send only the theme recorded in that
business's `faults` array, with its dated evidence. No fault recorded → no
mailer. A business gets one card, not a set.

## Anatomy

### Front — the fault, felt

Full-bleed still from that theme's clip, at the **departure beat** — boots
turning away in the rain, the note lying in the empty lot, the bell alone on
the counter. One line of type: the theme message. Nothing else. No studio
logo larger than a footer mark, no explanation, no offer.

The front is a poster. It is doing what the scenes do: staging the loss without
naming a villain. Because the scene is object theatre — no faces, no lettering,
no plausible fake business — the front cannot be read as mockery of anyone, and
the recipient's own site is not on it.

### Reverse — in this order, and the order is the point

1. **One true good thing about them**, from the verification record, in their
   own words where the record has words. *"Building in this county since 1973 —
   thirteen projects, from St Patrick's to Knockevin."*
2. **The fault, about the page only, and checkable.** *"We went looking for
   that record on your website on 6 August and could not find it on any single
   screen; the accreditations are in the footer."* Dated, describing what is
   there, no adjective about quality.
3. **The fix in one sentence**, plus one named concept as proof, carrying the
   independent-concept label the site uses. *"One register, filterable, all
   thirteen in a single view — the shape we built for [concept], an independent
   concept, not commissioned."*
4. **QR → the theme's own anchor** on `/what-we-look-for/#t7`, so the card and
   the page make the same argument rather than two.
5. **The offer:** free before-and-after, no obligation, what we need from them
   (a link), and the studio's local address.
6. **Small print:** generated-imagery disclosure for the front still, and the
   read date.

Leading with the good thing before the fault is not politeness — it is the
recognition move from the elevation method, done on paper. It is also the only
version that is *true*: every one of the sixty faults was a business doing
something well and a page failing to show it.

## Tone rules

Non-negotiable, and they follow the workstream's working agreement 7 (humour
punches up or sideways, never at owners for lacking a website):

- The fault is stated about **the page**, never the business, never the owner.
- **No competitor is named or implied**, ever. No "unlike others in town".
- **No score, no grade, no count.** Not "six of ten", not "your site scores".
- **No unmeasurable loss.** Never "you're losing customers" or "this is costing
  you bookings" — the studio has not measured that and never will from outside.
- **Nothing about age as a proxy for quality.** T9's card is about the page's
  clock, not the business's era.
- Dry, specific, local. If a line would be embarrassing read aloud to the
  recipient's face in the shop, it does not go on the card.

## The ten cards

Message = the theme message from the taxonomy brief. "Opens with" = the good
thing the card leads on, drawn from the record; the phrasings below are shapes,
not copy, because the actual sentence must come from that business's record.

| Card | Front still | Opens with (their good thing) | The page fault, checkably | Wrinkle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **T1** | Frosted pane across the lit door | What they plainly do well, in their own caption's words | Their own sentence is behind a consent dialog and a login panel for anyone not signed in | Recipients have **no site at all**. The fault must be about *reachability*, never about not having bothered. This is the card most likely to offend if written carelessly |
| **T2** | Paper note in the empty lot | The address people already know, on the van, the stationery, the cached search result | That address currently resolves to nothing / to another company's page | If their domain is dead, **the QR is the only route back** — it is load-bearing, print it large and test it |
| **T3** | Shields shrinking off the fascia | The mark, badge or name they chose themselves | On the first screen, the supplier marks are larger than theirs | Never disparage the supplier. The concepts *kept* them, in one confident line |
| **T4** | Umbrella turning away in the rain | That they publish their hours in full — most don't | Those hours sit N screens down, under a translate bar and a video | Safe to send; the errand is checkable in ten seconds by the owner themselves |
| **T5** | The bell that will not ring | That they answer the phone and the inbox properly | The Book page offers a number to ring, not a way to ask | Sympathetic framing: the phone works, the website makes the customer do the work |
| **T6** | Clipboards closing over the loaf | The thing at the end of the counter — the actual product | The first thing a visitor meets is the newsletter / the translate widget / the cancellation notes | Every one of these was somebody following advice. Say so |
| **T7** | Brass medal rising to the windowsill | The strongest true thing in the whole record — the years, the award, the arc | It is in the footer, or on no screen at all | **The best card in the set.** It is almost entirely a compliment |
| **T8** | The concertina unfolding down the door | That the information is all published — nothing is missing | It is a file / a wall of town names / a maker list, so it can't be read on a phone | Pairs with a measured line if a journey audit exists |
| **T9** | Dust on the stopped clock, steam on the loaf | That the business is visibly, currently thriving — this month's post proves it | The page still says an older year beside it | Hardest to write kindly. About the clock, never the era |
| **T10** | The plate that will not seat | Three specific true details only they have | — | **Not a mailer.** This is the leave-behind for a conversation, and the card for a business too well-run to have a fault worth posting |

## Format and production

- **A6, 350gsm, double-sided, matte** for posted and door-dropped volume. The
  front still is the whole front; the reverse takes six short blocks.
- **A5 variant for hand-delivery**, where the reverse has room for the **errand
  walk** — the same two-column before/after the transformation pages use
  (`TransformationErrand`: taps and screens, both sides). Only for businesses
  where a journey audit was actually run. This is the existing one-sheet
  convention at postcard scale.
- **Generated stills in print carry the same obligations as on the site:** a
  provenance entry in `research/image-provenance.md` before the artwork is
  committed, and a visible disclosure line on the card. Print is not an
  exemption.
- **The independent-concept label survives onto paper.** Any named concept on a
  card says it was uncommissioned, in type a reader will actually see.
- No invented testimonial, ranking, client or result appears on any card.

## Tracking

The batch selection doc (`research/pipeline/batch-*-selection.md`) gains a
**postcard** column: which theme was sent, on what date, from which recorded
fault. Two reasons — a second contact must not repeat the first card, and if a
card's claim is ever questioned, the chain runs card → batch doc → `faults`
entry → dated source, with no gap.

## Open questions

- Is the departure still legible as a *front* with no context, or does the front
  need the message plus a second line? Decide from the printed proofs, not on
  screen — these are 105 × 148 mm.
- Does the T1 card work at all, or is "you have no website" unsendable by post
  and only ever said in person?
- Should the QR land on the theme anchor or on `/request/` pre-filled with the
  theme? The anchor teaches; the pre-fill converts. Try both in one batch.
- One card per business is the rule. Does a business with four recorded faults
  get the strongest, or the most fixable?
