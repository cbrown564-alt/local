# Concept essence media brief

This document owns the signature-media idea for each concept site. It answers
one question: **what image, short film or small image sequence could make the
character of this business felt before the page has to explain it?**

The working reference is the Cúpla twin-pour film: two cups, two jugs, one
locked composition and one satisfying change from black coffee to finished
flat whites. It is specific to the business without showing the premises or
trying to tell the whole story.

## The standard

- Express the business's particular truth, not merely its trade or property.
- Prefer one visual idea and one legible change. A six-to-eight-second film or
  three-to-five composition-matched images is usually enough.
- Keep the camera and scene restrained. The concept should survive as a strong
  poster frame.
- Use real products, people, premises and claims only when they are sourced and
  cleared. Otherwise use faithful visualisation, obvious illustration or
  symbolic objects that cannot be mistaken for documentary evidence.
- Generated media remains provisional. Before any asset ships or is committed,
  add its source, method and disclosure decision to
  [`research/image-provenance.md`](image-provenance.md).

## Concepts

Use a sequence when the meaning depends on accumulated work, distinct eras or
several documented stages. Use film when one physical action can carry the
whole idea. A sequence follows the useful part of the X23 method—one locked
composition and controlled changes—but must be grounded in the business's
real geography or record. X23 itself was retired because its fictional town
weakened the local-trust claim.

## Production order

Prove the pattern with Cúpla in the actual page first. Then make one example
from each different register before producing the whole set:

1. **Product transformation:** Betty's Better Butters or Scopers.
2. **Place and day-part:** The Dundrum Inn or Hotel Enniskeen.
3. **Service and reassurance:** Donard Veterinary Clinic or Newcastle Family
   Dental Care.
4. **History in motion:** Kent Amusements or Hugh McCann's.
5. **Diagrammatic connection:** Newcastle Chamber of Commerce.

For each, judge the loop in its real crop on mobile and desktop, check the
poster frame and reduced-motion still, and only then extract a reusable
production template.
