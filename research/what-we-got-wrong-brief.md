# `/what-we-got-wrong/` — the retired work, published

*Written 14 August 2026. The public surface for the decisions currently held in
`PROSPECTS.md` "Retired concepts", `PLAN.md` §1a–1c, `research/publication.json`
and the ADRs. Every entry on this page already exists in the repository, dated
and reasoned; nothing here asks for new research. Sibling in posture to
[`what-we-look-for-brief.md`](what-we-look-for-brief.md) — that page points the
sentence at the studio instead of the reader, and this one finishes the
gesture.*

## The problem this page solves

`PRODUCT.md` sets the belief sequence: the studio understands this place → the
route can be clearer → the change is concrete → the scope fits → requesting is
worth doing now. Eighteen transformations carry rungs two and three well.

Rung one is carried almost entirely by prose, and rung five is blocked by a
fact stated plainly in `PRODUCT.md`: **no business has approved the work and no
client result has been measured.** That is the honest position and it will stay
the position until a pilot closes. Meanwhile the portfolio has the shape of
every studio portfolio — eighteen wins, no losses — and the swap test we apply
to everyone else applies to us: replace *Mourne Made* with any competitor's
name on `/transformations/`, and the page still works.

Replace the studio's name on this page and it does not work, because nobody
else has these failures. **It is the only page on the site that cannot be
swapped**, and it is made entirely of material already written.

## The name does the tonal work

Not `/failures/` — a noun, and it invites the reader to weigh whether we are
any good. Not `/lessons-learned/` — the phrase belongs to a corporate
retrospective and signals that nothing costly is about to be admitted. Not
`/where-we-failed/` — too close a rhyme with `/where-it-fails/`, and the two
pages would read as a matched pair of self-abasement and criticism.

**`/what-we-got-wrong/`** is plain, past tense and finished. Past tense
matters: it promises each entry ends in a change, which is the rule the whole
page is built on.

Working headline: **Four concepts we stopped, and what stopping them changed.**
Subhead: the work is uncommissioned, so the only proof we can offer is how we
behave when we are wrong.

## The one rule that keeps this from being humility theatre

**Every entry names what changed as a result. A failure with no consequence is
an anecdote, and an anecdote about our own honesty is an advertisement.**

Applied strictly, that rule does most of the editorial work: it admits Tonn
Ruray (a check was rewritten), admits the census collapse (a claim was retired
and a glossary was written), admits the test-suite regressions (the runner was
rebuilt), and excludes anything we merely feel bad about. If an entry's "what
changed" line is soft, the entry is not ready to publish.

Second rule, subordinate to the first: **no entry may be a humblebrag in
disguise.** "We were too thorough" is not an entry. The Tonn Ruray entry passes
because the failure is that we built something worse than what existed and four
checks let it through. That is not flattering in any reading.

## Who may be named

This is the honesty problem specific to this page, and it is not the same one
`/where-it-fails/` solved. There, the rule was simple: no real business appears
next to a fault, ever — recorded faults go out one-to-one on a postcard. Here
the failures are *ours*, but four of them have a real business standing next to
them.

The test: **would the business, reading the entry, find a claim about itself
that it did not make public and would not welcome?**

| Entry | Naming | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tonn Ruray Café | **Name them** | The entry's content is that their site is better than what we proposed: their own shopfront photograph in low golden light, their own food photography, a quote they chose because they liked it, a hand-drawn squiggle under *See the Menu*. It is praise, and it is about published public design. |
| Groves Chemist | **Do not name** | The entry rests on the pharmacy having come off a centralised platform, which is a fact about their commercial arrangements that they did not publish about themselves. Publish as *a pharmacy* and keep the lesson, which is entirely ours: we wrote the design task as *restore the capability that was switched off*, presuming they wanted it back. |
| Kelly, McEvoy & Brown | **Name them** | Already public at `/transformations/kelly-mcevoy-brown/`. The failure is that our case-study premise about their portfolio was false and their real site was better than we had described. Correcting a false claim about a business in public is owed to them, not a risk to them. |
| Painted Earth | **Name them** | Already public, and the disagreement is already recorded in `PROSPECTS.md` in these terms. The entry says the reviewer recommended stopping and the project owner published anyway — a disagreement between two of us about our own work. |
| The Buck's Head | **Name them, quote nothing** | Their reply was a private email from a director. Publish only the studio's conclusion — our first outreach went to a business that had already solved the problem, so the pitch was wrong and the business was fine — and never the correspondence, the director, or the unlaunched site's timing. If in any doubt, ask them first. |

Where a business is named, that business may ask to be removed from this page
and is removed. Same rule as `/the-lights/`, same day, no questions.

## The entries

Six, in two registers: four about the work, two about the machinery. All six
already exist in the repo with dates and reasoning — the drafting job is
compression, not research.

### 1. We built something worse than what we proposed to replace

**Tonn Ruray Café, Dundrum. Retired 27 July 2026.**

The concept deleted the owners' photography for typography on cream, compressed
a quote they had chosen into a grotesk slogan and credited it back to them,
replaced their dusty-pink and italic-serif identity with the studio's, rendered
all seven links as struck-through dead placeholders, and printed a remark about
their own room pricing inside the page being offered to them.

The real gap on that site was a menu PDF stamped JUN24 and a *BOOK NOW* that
resolved to a contact form. **Those are maintenance problems, and a redesign
does not fix them.**

*What changed:* the elevation method's fifth check — *is this better than what
they already have* — is now answered against the live site at phone and desktop
before publication, not from the brief. It is the check that caught Kelly,
McEvoy & Brown and it is the check that Painted Earth was published in spite
of.

### 2. All four checks passed it anyway

Same concept, separate entry, because it is a different failure and the more
important one. Four publication checks were run on Tonn Ruray and all four
passed. The concept was retired by someone looking at it, not by the process.

A false claim was also found during the retirement: the published case study
and the before-clip alt text both stated the homepage opened on *Sea View
Luxury Apartments* with the café beneath. **Our own published capture shows the
homepage opening on the café shopfront, `EAT` first in the navigation, and no
apartments anywhere in the captured scroll.** It was the fourth false sourcing
claim of a class we had swept for the day before, and the sweep had missed it.

*What changed:* five checks, not four. Provenance is classified from
`research/image-provenance.md` rather than from the filename and alt text the
check then asserted against — which had made it tautological. Alt text no
longer counts as disclosure, because it is not what a sighted visitor is told.

### 3. We offered to rebuild something they had already switched off

**A pharmacy in Dundrum and Killough. Retired 27 July 2026.**

The design task was written as *restore the capability that was switched off*.
The evidence established only that it stopped — not whether they opted out, let
it lapse, or had it retired for them. **Offering to rebuild it is offering work
they may have already declined.**

*What changed:* a gap in the evidence is no longer a gap in the business. Gap
class selection now has to state what the evidence supports rather than what
absence suggests.

### 4. The reviewer said stop and we published anyway

**Painted Earth, Newcastle. Published 3 August 2026.**

The review recommended stopping it: the shop's strength is photography of real
work by 92 named makers, none of it licensed to us, so every artwork in the
concept is a drawn placeholder tile — and on *is this better than what they
already have*, the live gallery wins on imagery. The project owner published it
anyway, on the grounds that the tiles are visibly labelled and the loop around
them carries enough on its own.

Both positions are on the page. **We are not claiming the reviewer was right;
we are claiming the disagreement is on the record**, which is the part most
studios would delete.

*What changed:* nothing in the process, and the entry says so. Disagreements
between the reviewer and the project owner are now recorded as disagreements
rather than settled silently — this is that record, made public.

### 5. Our first outreach went to a business that had already fixed it

**The Buck's Head, Dundrum. Replied 27 July 2026.**

A concept, a published transformation and a printed one-sheet, aimed at a
business that had already built its replacement website. Recorded internally as
*no current opportunity*, not as a rejection, and the pitch was stopped.

*What changed:* the sheet was not sent, the batch was not topped back up to
three to preserve its size, and Hotel Enniskeen — the highest-stakes
conversation — stays in batch two so it is not used to rehearse the approach.

### 6. The claim we most wanted to make did not survive its own data

**"Most local businesses here have no website." Retired 5 August 2026.**

It was going to be the centrepiece of a sitewide component. Of 379 census rows,
281 were flagged as having no site — a set that included a golf course, ten
national chains, the police station, the fire station, a primary school, two
graveyards, four walking trails and a road called *High Mournes scenic loop*.
246 of the 281 were marked as never independently confirmed.

Then we checked sixteen of the remaining dark businesses by hand and found ten
that had a website the census had missed — a solicitor, a vet, an optician, a
shoe shop, a pizza place with online ordering. **Our own flag was wrong about
two times in three.** Published as it stood, the map would have been wrong
about roughly a hundred real businesses in a town of 7,500.

The root cause was a glossary gap. *Prospect* was defined as "a business
assessed against the census", and *census* was never defined — so a scraped
catalogue of listed places was read as a list of businesses.

*What changed:* `docs/CONTEXT.md` now defines **Census**, **Trading business**
and **Lit / dark**. A four-step website hunt ran over every dark mapped trader
and took unconfirmed rows from 150 to 0. The honest figure is **85 of 166 —
about half, never "most"** — and it is what `/the-lights/` publishes.

### 7. We deleted the tests that were protecting the flagship

**4 August 2026.** Eleven browser suites and the journey orchestrator were
removed because a full run took 45+ minutes in CI and often timed out after
passing. The decision stands — pre-first-sale, that cost was not justified.

What went with them was not evenly distributed. `PLAN.md` §1c carries the table
of what is no longer covered, and three rows say *nothing replaces it*,
including the homepage claim-door link — the only route from the homepage to a
business's own concept, which **had already gone missing once** in a redesign
before a test caught it.

*What changed:* the run-every-suite runner was rebuilt, the abort-on-first-
failure chaining that had twice hidden passing-by-omission was removed again,
and the printed-QR attribution check was rewritten without a browser. Every new
assertion was confirmed to fail under mutation before being wired in — because
an earlier pass had shipped two checks that could not fail. The uncovered rows
are still uncovered and the table stays public.

## Page structure

```
1  Opening         What this page is. One paragraph: the work is
                   uncommissioned, no client result has been measured,
                   so the only proof available is how we behave when
                   we are wrong. State it once, plainly, and do not
                   return to it.

2  The entries     Seven blocks, in the order above — work first,
                   machinery second. Each block:

                     the failure          (one line, in the title)
                     what happened        (three to five sentences)
                     what changed         (one line, always present)
                     the date             (always)

                   No before/after control. No imagery of the retired
                   concepts — they were deleted, and reconstructing
                   them to display them would be the same mistake with
                   a second coat.

3  What we would   The two things currently uncovered and known:
   still get       the §1c test gaps, and the concepts published on
   wrong           labelled placeholders. Naming an open weakness is
                   the difference between a confession and a habit.

4  Close           → /request/, quietly. The argument has already
                   been made by section 2; a hard sell here would
                   undo it.
```

**No illustration.** The three explainer pages earn their drawn plates because
they teach a mechanism. This page teaches nothing — it is a record — and
illustrating a retirement would decorate it. Set in the same type grid as the
assembly chassis, dated entries, no ornament. The plainness is the design.

## Where this page sits

Not in the primary navigation. It is linked from three places where a reader
has just been given a reason to doubt us:

- `/about/` — after the local commitment.
- `/transformations/` — at the foot of the index, as *the ones that are not
  here*. This is the strongest placement: a portfolio index that links to its
  own retirements is making a claim no other studio site makes.
- `/the-lights/` §4 — the entry about the census collapse is the reason that
  page's number is believable.

## Build notes

- Route `/what-we-got-wrong/`. One component, seven data entries — the same
  discipline as `fault-walks.ts` and `five-shapes.ts`. Seven bespoke blocks
  would be seven bespoke bugs.
- Each entry is `{ id, title, date, body[], changed, business? }`. `business`
  is optional and absent for the pharmacy by design, so an anonymised entry
  cannot be de-anonymised by a later edit that fills the field in.
- **Pin the naming rule.** A static check over `dist/` asserting that no entry
  whose `business` field is absent contains a business name from
  `businesses.json` in its body. This is the assertion most likely to save us,
  because the failure mode is a helpful edit six months from now.
- Pin that every entry carries both a date and a non-empty `changed` — the
  rule the page is built on, enforced rather than remembered.
- Source of truth stays `PROSPECTS.md`, `PLAN.md` and `publication.json`. The
  page is a rendering of decisions, not a second place they are made. If an
  entry cannot cite the internal record it came from, it is not an entry.
- No generated media, no provenance entries, no disclosures needed.

## Open questions

- Is seven too many? Entries 1 and 2 are one concept; 6 and 7 are machinery. A
  reader who bounces at four has still had the argument. Prefer cutting 7
  first — it is the most inside-baseball and the least legible to an owner.
- Does the Buck's Head entry survive legal-instinct review? It names a business
  in connection with an unlaunched website. The safest version names nobody and
  loses little: *our first outreach went to a business that had already built
  its replacement.* Consider defaulting to that.
- Does this page ever get *harder* to write? Once a pilot exists, a failure
  entry could be about a paying client. The rule should probably be written
  now, before it costs anything: client work appears here only with the
  client's written agreement, and never as the only version of events.
- Should the page carry a date of its own — *last added to* — so a reader can
  see it is maintained rather than a one-off gesture? Probably yes, and it
  creates an obligation we should mean.
