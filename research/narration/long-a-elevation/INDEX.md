## What to listen for

1. **The act break at S5.** The method's own recorded failure — hotel moves
   applied to a food bar — sits in the middle where an act break belongs. If
   the piece has a weak point structurally, it is whether S4 hands over to S5
   with enough of a turn, or whether it just continues.
2. **S6 and S7 are the longest sections and the most list-like.** Five shapes,
   then six tests. That is where a listener's attention is most likely to go.
   If either drags, the fix is cutting an item, not re-reading it.
3. **The close.** "Beautiful, he could have bought anywhere" is the last line
   and the whole argument compressed. It needs the beat before it to land.

## Choices worth knowing

**No business is named anywhere in it.** Every example is a shape — *a country
house hotel*, *a zero-waste hot food bar*, *a shop whose shelf was never
stocked*. This goes past the §3 place-name rule on purpose: it closes the last
route to identifying anyone, and it lets an owner hear their own kind of
business rather than a neighbour's. The Scopers review is the piece's turn and
is told entirely as "a food bar" without losing anything.

**Shorter than costed** — ~7,600 characters against 9,400 budgeted, 8:31
against eleven minutes. The cut came from reading aloud: the removed passages
were the ones that *summarised* the source document rather than dramatising it,
and they were unsayable. This is the §1 claim about script discipline working
as intended, and it leaves ~4,600 credits unspent against this piece.

**The paradoxes are verbatim from the source.** The chef and the milk bread
buns, the unstocked shelf, nine years old and still "newly opened", the dealer
at a Gmail address. They were already the strongest writing in the repo; audio
did not need to improve them, only to leave space around them.
