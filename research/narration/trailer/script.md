**Job:** the thing you play first. Establish that there is a method here, and
that it has teeth. Ends pointing at the work, not at a form.

**Sources.** Cold open is T1 from
[`../../film/studio-recurring-themes.md`](../../film/studio-recurring-themes.md)

**Honesty check.** No invented client, no testimonial, no ranking, no measured
result. The one moment that reached for a prevalence claim — *"that happened
this morning, it will happen again this afternoon"* — was cut in drafting: it
asserts a frequency nobody counted. What replaced it says only what is
certainly true, that nobody reports a failure like this, and lands harder for
being unarguable.

**No place names**, per §3. "Round here" does the work that a town name would.

## Script

*1,134 characters, 1:35.6.*

Someone is standing outside your door, on their phone.

[pause]

They want to know if you're open.

So they search your name. And what comes back is a login screen. Your name is
behind it — greyed out, underneath a box asking them to accept cookies.

They tap it. Nothing. They tap it again.

[pause]

Then they go next door.

Nobody will ever tell you that happened. There's no complaint, no bad review,
no email. Just a quiet person, outside, who came all the way to your door and
left.

[reflective]

We've looked closely at twenty businesses round here. Not their marketing —
their websites. On a phone, in the rain, in a hurry.

Roughly sixty things were wrong. Ten patterns account for nearly all of them.

Not one of them is that the site looked ugly.

[pause]

Here's the one that stings.

Open your website. Find your name. Now swap it for the business up the street.

[pause]

If nothing else on the page needs changing... the site isn't yours. It's a
template with your name in the corner.

[matter-of-fact]

A site that's yours knows what the person outside came to ask.

That's the work.

We'll show you yours before we ask you for anything.

## Script as first read

*1,382 characters, 2:04.4. Kept for the comparison.*

Someone is standing outside your door, on their phone.

[pause]

They're not lost. They know what they want. They want to know if you're open.

So they search your name. And what comes back is a login screen. Your name is
behind it — greyed out, underneath a box asking them to accept cookies.

They tap it. Nothing. They tap it again.

[pause]

Then they go next door.

Nobody will ever tell you that happened. That's the thing about it. There's no
complaint, no bad review, no email. Just a quiet person, outside, who came all
the way to your door and left.

[reflective]

We've looked closely at twenty businesses round here. Not their marketing —
their websites. The way a stranger meets them. On a phone, in the rain, in a
hurry.

Roughly sixty things were wrong. And read together, they repeat. Ten patterns
account for nearly all of them.

Not one of them is that the site looked ugly.

[pause]

Here's the one that stings.

Open your website. Find your name. Now swap it for the business up the street.

[pause]

If nothing else on the page needs changing... the site isn't yours. It's a
template with your name in the corner. And people can feel that, even if
they'd never say it out loud.

[matter-of-fact]

A site that's actually yours knows the one thing a template never can. What
the person outside came to ask.

That's the work.

We'll show you yours before we ask you for anything.
