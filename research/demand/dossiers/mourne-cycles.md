## The problem we think they have

Three jobs a bike shop earns from are invisible: booking a service, seeing
what's in stock at roughly what price, and knowing the shop does Cycle to
Work. A rider searching "bike service Newcastle" or "Cycle to Work Co Down"
finds nothing to act on.

## Would they want it?

- **For:** the workshop is steady, repeatable revenue, and a service
  request is a clear measure. Cycle to Work sales are high value.
- **Against:** a long-established shop may have all the workshop trade it
  can handle, booked by phone and by regulars. Trek dealers sometimes get a
  brand-run site or store locator, which may make them feel covered.
- **Kill question:** "How far ahead is the workshop booked right now?"

## Pitch to test

> "People looking for a service or Cycle to Work can't tell from your site
> that you do either. I made a version where they can send the bike's
> details and pick a day, and you confirm. Nothing live, no diary to keep."

## Smallest paid scope and the one measure

**Direct action:** a workshop request form that sends service type and
preferred day to their inbox, a Cycle to Work page, and an accurate Google
profile. The full six-page concept only if they want the range online.
Measure: workshop requests through the site per month.

## Timing

**October–November** is workshop season (bikes in for winter servicing),
and a slower retail period, so there's time to talk. The next natural
point is **February–March**, when riders prepare for spring.

## Who decides

The owner, probably in the shop. Avoid Saturdays.

## Step 0 before the first visit

- Re-check the Wix site and any Trek-provided dealer page.
- Check their Cyclescheme listing is current.
- Search "bike service Newcastle Co Down" on a phone and note what comes
  up first.
