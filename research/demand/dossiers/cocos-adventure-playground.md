## The problem we think they have

A parent deciding on a wet Saturday morning can't tell from the web
whether Coco's is open today, and a parent planning a party has to phone
during opening hours to ask whether a date is free. Both are moments a
family goes somewhere else instead.

## Would they want it?

- **For:** parties are a real booking with a real value (£100 minimum, by
  their own prices). Winter and wet days are their busy season. The audit
  flagged it independently.
- **Against:** walk-in play is already "no booking necessary", and they may
  be happy with phone bookings for parties.
- **Kill question:** "How do party bookings reach you now, and do you ever
  miss one because nobody could answer?"

## Pitch to test

> "Parents trust Coco's; the thing they can't find online is whether you're
> open today and whether their party date is free. I made a page that
> answers both before they pick up the phone."

## Smallest paid scope and the one measure

**Presence repair plus direct action:** an accurate hours block they can
change from a phone, and a party-date request that reaches their inbox
with date, package and number of children. Measure: party requests per
month and how many become bookings.

## Timing

**Now.** The Halloween half-term (late October) and Christmas parties are
the next peaks. Parties are booked weeks ahead, so a request form live in
October catches December.

## Who decides

Owner-operated; the owner is probably on site. Visit mid-morning on a
school day.
