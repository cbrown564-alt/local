# Discovery conversation guide

For the first conversation with each of the ten businesses in the
[demand workstream](README.md). Fifteen minutes, in person, at a quiet time
of their day. The aim is to learn how customers reach them and what that
costs. It is not to sell the concept.

## Before you go in

- Do the desk re-check in the dossier's **Step 0**. Know what their site and
  social page show *today*.
- Know the one failure you believe they have, and be ready to be wrong.
- Pick the time: after the lunch rush for food, mid-morning mid-week for
  accommodation, never on a changeover day or at a wedding.
- Bring a phone with the concept loaded, and the one-sheet if one exists.
  Keep both out of sight until the end.

## Opening (30 seconds)

> "I'm Conor, I run a small web studio in Dundrum. I'm not here to sell you
> anything today. I'm trying to understand how places around here actually
> get their bookings, and whether the web helps or gets in the way. Could I
> ask you a few questions? Ten minutes."

If they're busy, ask when would suit and leave. Don't leave material behind
unless they ask for it.

## Questions

Ask about the past, not hypotheticals. "When did that last happen?" beats
"Would you use…?" every time. Listen for numbers and for irritation.

**How customers reach them**

1. "Where did your last few [bookings / parties / wedding enquiries /
   new patients] come from?"
2. "What do people usually ask when they first get in touch?"
3. "How do they get in touch: phone, Facebook message, email,
   Booking.com, walk in?"

**What it costs**

4. "What happens when someone wants to book at 10pm?"
5. "How much time goes on the phone answering the same questions?"
6. For accommodation: "Roughly what share of your nights come through
   Booking.com or Airbnb? Do you know what that costs you a year?"
7. "Has anyone ever told you they couldn't find something online: your
   hours, prices, a date?"

**What they've tried**

8. "Who looks after your website and Facebook now?"
9. "Have you ever had a website built? How did that go?"
10. "Have you thought about changing any of it? What stopped you?"

**Timing and deciding**

11. "When's your quiet time, when you'd have headspace for something like
    this?"
12. "If you did change something, who else would have a say?"

## Showing the concept (only if they described the problem)

> "I actually made something along these lines, as an independent idea.
> Nobody asked me to and there's no obligation. Can I show you?"

Show the one screen that answers the problem they just described, not the
whole site. Then stop talking and watch. Note the first thing they say.

Then one question: **"If that existed, what would it change for you?"**

## Close

- If they're interested: "What would be a sensible next step for you?" Let
  them name it. If they ask the price, give the smallest scope and say
  you'll put a fixed figure in writing.
- If not: "That's really useful, thank you. Is it all right if I leave my
  number?" Nothing more.

## Listen for

| They say | It tells you |
|---|---|
| A number ("about thirty a month", "fifteen percent") | Rung 3. Write it down verbatim |
| "We've just had it done" / "my nephew does it" | Solved, or a relationship in the way. Ask how it's going |
| "People just ring" | Maybe the phone *is* their system. Ask what they miss when they can't answer |
| "Facebook works for us" | Often true for news. Ask about the thing Facebook can't hold: prices, terms, availability |
| "How much?" | Rung 4. Don't guess a number on the spot |
| Irritation about a platform (commission, algorithm, login wall) | The real problem is probably there, not in the design |

## Avoid

- Saying their current site is bad, old or broken. Describe the job it
  can't do.
- Hypotheticals ("Would you pay for…?"). People say yes to be kind.
- Talking about AI, automation, dashboards or tools.
- Promising outcomes ("more bookings"). We have no client results yet.
- Staying longer than you said.

## Straight after (in the car, not later)

Write in the dossier's **Log**: date, who (by role), rung reached, the
problem in their words, any number, objections, the agreed next step and
when. Then update the `demand` block in `verifications.json` (see the
README).
