## The insight the first pass missed

The record's strangeness is not that the clinic renamed itself. It is
**everything that refused to change while the name changed twice.** Marine
Sports Massage Therapy (search-title era) became Marine Beauty Haven at
91 Central Promenade became Marine Wellness at Unit 13, 63A Castlewellan
Road — and through all of it, the same phone number (028 4372 6207), the
same Facebook page (the vanity URL still reads `marinebeauty.haven` —
the old name, fossilised in the link itself), the same Booksy diary, and
the same hands: Patricia, twenty-plus years in.

Put yourself in the client's shoes — the person this page is actually for.
She had a massage at Marine Beauty Haven on the Promenade a year or two ago.
She searches for it now and finds "Marine Wellness" on an industrial-unit
road she has never visited, while Bing insists the Beauty Haven is still on
Central Promenade. Her one question is not "what treatments do they offer?"
It is: **is this the same place? Did my clinic move, or did it close?**

The live web answers that question badly — directories lag, the site's
title tag still reads *Your Site Title*, the Facebook link still carries the
old name. The concept's job is to answer it in one breath: **same hands,
same number, new name, new room.** That is the reassurance this register
asks for, and no spa template has it, because no template knows the clinic
moved.

The second thing the first pass left on the table: the people *are* the arc.
Patricia McGrady, over twenty years. Nicola, over ten. Emma, three and
counting. A clinic that grows by decades of the same hands — no founding
year is published, and none is needed: the tenures are the timeline.

## The essence

**Same hands. Same number. New name, new room.**

A concept that books on a fake calendar fails Booksy. A concept that keeps
Beauty Haven on the door fails the rename. A concept that never mentions
the rename fails the returning client — the one person who needs the page
most. The elevated page is the clinic's continuity, stated once, calmly,
and then the door.

1. **"We do not publish hours" is false.** The site publishes a full table.
   The one piece of practical reassurance a relief page can give — when the

2. **The continuity story is unsaid.** The rename is the client's actual
   question, and the page's only nod to it is a bare Facebook link that

3. **The people are missing.** Patricia's name sits in a kicker; Nicola and

4. **Booksy's own booking rules are unused.** Deposit, 24-hour cancellation,

   drafts words for Booksy but never sets expectations.
5. **No voice, no grade.** One plate, studio-grey copy, no carry of the
