## 1. The shape of this business

A **Product & Maker Studio (Jar / Studio)** with a promenade café shelf. It is neither a generic classroom nor a standard seaside café:

- **What the customer buys**: A tangible piece of handmade art, the tactile joy of working with raw clay/glazes, and an afternoon/evening overlooking Dundrum Bay.
- **The paradox**: A buzzing, bookable 2026 workshop diary (Make a Mug, Charcuterie Night, Autumn Flora) with in-house porcelain production and zero published phone number.
- **The register**: Coastal craft, tactile warmth, natural light, and artisan patience.

The incumbent mediums are split: Squarespace holds the workshop ticket sales; Instagram showcases the aesthetic room and smiling guests; Google holds the promenade pin (4.9★ / 29 reviews). The concept wins by uniting the physical seaside studio presence, the tactile beauty of handcrafted porcelain, and direct workshop seat reservations without generic builder clutter.

Introduce an interactive **"Bisque to Kiln" spectrum**: a horizontal tactile rail showcasing the transformation of raw Mourne clay → Adrian's bisque ware → hand-painted botanicals → fired porcelain with high-gloss glaze. Guests see and understand the 3-week firing cycle.

### Move 4: The live workshop ledger & BYOB evening cards
