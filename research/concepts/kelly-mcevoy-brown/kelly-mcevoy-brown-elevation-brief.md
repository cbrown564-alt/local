## The shape of this business

Kelly, McEvoy & Brown is a **trade business selling to procurement**:
what the customer buys is not a building but confidence, before a tender
— the accreditations checked, the record scanned for a building like the
one being planned, the phone number taken. The theatre register is
**expertise made visible**: not the ride, not the plate, but the record
itself, shown whole. The owner test has two sitters: the partner who
opens the link and wants the firm seen as the fifty-three-year practice
it is, and the committee member or quantity surveyor who arrives cold and
needs the evidence in one screen. The concept already serves the second
better than the live site does — four clicks to one building, against
one register — and serves the first barely at all.

The incumbent medium here is not a feed but the firm's own hand-built
site, and the 3 August re-review is the standing lesson: the concept
competes with what kmbni.com actually is, not with what a first read
assumed it was. Every claim this brief makes about the live site has been
checked against it twice; nothing below requires the live site to be
worse than it is.

## The essence

**One of Northern Ireland's most respected construction companies keeps
its record in the smallest site in the portfolio: fifty-three years of
churches, hospitals, schools and community halls, from Magheralin to
Crossmaglen, in 9.7 KB of hand-built HTML.**

The paradox is the weight differential: the work is physical, monumental
and scattered across the counties; the site is tiny, dated and — by the
firm's own choice of it — enough. The concept's job is not to fix the
firm's site but to show what the record looks like when it is treated as
the asset it is. The angle is inside the paradox: the record is the
identity. "Build on experience" is not a slogan the page borrows; it is
the literal contents of the portfolio.

## The diagnosis

1. **The record is a list, not a shape.** The register holds all thirteen
   projects in one view — the concept's genuine improvement — but a
   filtered list cannot show what fifty-three years of work looks like
   spread across a patch: the reach from Magheralin to Crossmaglen, the
   weight of ecclesiastical and healthcare work, the school in the firm's
   own town.
2. **The swap test passes on identity and fails on structure.** Strip the
   mark, the palette and the two photographs — all genuinely the firm's —
   and what remains is a filterable list any contractor could wear. The
   map of built work is the element that could only be this firm's.
3. **The arc is a titleblock.** "Established 1973" sits as a kicker pair
   beside "Six sectors"; the fifty-three years the headline claims are
   nowhere made visible.
4. **The accreditation wall is present but mute.** Five marks in a row
   state that the firm is accredited; nothing says what the marks are
   *for* — the procurement evidence they constitute — although the public
   case study already names it.
5. **Two repair cycles is the context, not a criticism.** Invented
   drawing numbers, inferred places, a fabricated chip, an identity
   blocker, a false case-study premise — all fixed, all recorded. The
   next pass adds to the page from the verified record and changes no
   existing claim. Quiet is a requirement, not a style.

## The moves

Ordered by the recommended build sequence: voice first, then the essence
made visible, then theatre, then the keepable artifact. Here the essence,
the theatre and the artifact converge on one plate, so the sequence is
short.

### 1. Finish the voice work, and nothing more

Small editorial, no new assets: "Build on experience" stays the h1 and
the firm's line stays the only slogan on the page; the arc fixture —
Established 1973, fifty-three years — holds its place beside it. Fold in
the publication record's flagged repair: the case-study `afterAlt` still
describes drawing numbers the concept no longer carries. No new claims,
no re-diagnosis, no premise changes anywhere.

### 2. Draw the map of built work

The essence made visible and the keepable artifact in one plate — the
contractor's equivalent of the estate plate: a drawn map of the patch,
sector-keyed, with the eleven published places pinned and the two
unplaced projects listed beside the plate rather than invented onto it.
Drawn in the concept's yellow-and-grey, grounded only in what the firm's
site publishes, disclosed "indicative · not a survey". The map shows what
no list can: the record as a territory — and it is as much for every
parish, trust and school board on it as for the firm. Budgeted as its own
drawing session per the method; reviewed at phone width for clipped
labels; the one-sheet takes the map on its reverse.

### 3. Stage the tender ritual

The accreditation wall told as what it is: the evidence pack. Five marks,
each with its plain-English weight for a client checking a contractor
before an invitation to tender, staged beside the firm's own phone and
email — the enquiry route the rebuild already carries. This is the B2B
recognition move: the page speaks procurement because the business runs
on it. Nothing claimed beyond what the site publishes — the marks as the
site lists them, the contact details as the header carries them.

### 4. Let the register drive the map

The theatre mechanism, built and tested before the plate arrives: the
sector filter that already drives the register also lights the map —
selecting Ecclesiastical brings its pins up and dims the rest. The
mechanism is built against a placeholder plate first, per the Enniskeen
lesson, so the drawing session drops assets into tested slots. Without
JavaScript the map shows every pin and the register shows every row —
the full record is the no-JS state, not a degraded one.

## Recommended bundles

- **The mechanism pass:** move 4's filter-drives-map behaviour against a

- **The drawing session:** move 2's plate, dropped into the tested

## Honesty constraints

- **Photography stays at two until rights are confirmed.** The blocked
  lever is respected, not worked around: no new project imagery, no stock
  of buildings the firm did not build, no image implied against a project
  the source page does not match — the provenance record's rule
  (`research/image-provenance.md`) stands.
- **Places come from the firm's site or its published project names,
  nowhere else.** The triage cycle removed inferred places once
  (Armagh, Saintfield-as-inference, Belfast); the map must not reintroduce
  the failure at plate scale. Projects without a published place are
  listed, not pinned. The plate carries "indicative · not a survey".
- **One date and the present.** 1973 is the only verified milestone; no
  founders, no project dates, no completion years, no scale or client
  claims beyond the site's own text.
- **No third surprise.** This pass adds; it does not re-diagnose. No
  changes to the case-study premise, the register's contents or the
  concept's identity work beyond what these moves state.
- **The accreditations are the firm's claims, staged as published** — no
  gloss on what any mark certifies beyond the generic procurement role
  the case study already states.
- **The same-name distinction stands** per the verification caveat; the
  concept remains specific to the Dundrum-registered firm.
- A drawn plate keeps the disclosure chain — alt text, visible note,
  case-study Sources & limits, same-commit provenance — and any motion in
  the filter mechanism respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Any change to the first viewport recaptures the comparison stills and
  clip in the same commit, per `docs/MEDIA_CAPTURE.md`.

## Tests that pin the elevation

- A standing scan asserts no drawing-number pattern appears anywhere on
  the concept or in its case study — the first triage failure pinned as a
  test, not a memory.
- Assert every pinned place matches the firm's published site text or a
  published project name, and that the two unplaced projects appear
  beside the plate and not on it; assert the indicative disclosure is
  present.
- Assert the only year strings on the page are 1973 and the arc line
  derived from it.
- Assert the sector filter drives register and map consistently — counts
  stay accurate per sector, the matching pins respond, and with
  JavaScript disabled every pin and every row is visible.
- Inspect the plate at 390px for clipped labels.
- Assert the case-study `afterAlt` no longer mentions drawing numbers.
- Run the guest-voice guard and the publication checks; then the swap
  test: remove the mark, the palette, the photographs and the thirteen
  names — if the page still works for any contractor, the elevation has
  not gone far enough.

## Open decisions

- The map's honesty rests on eleven of thirteen places being published.
  Is a plate with two projects standing beside it the right artifact, or
  does the map wait until the firm confirms locations — the same
  engagement that would unblock the photography? This brief's position:
  draw it now, disclosed; the two unpinned names beside the plate are an
  honest state, not a flaw.
- Does the filter-drives-map mechanism earn its JavaScript on a B2B page
  whose no-JS state must be the full record? The mechanism is theatre for
  the partner; the register alone serves the surveyor. Review at build.
- The publication record names a sector page as the natural second
  surface "if this becomes a flagship". This brief holds that: the map is
  the second surface, and a sector page follows only if outreach — not
  yet begun — turns the concept into a flagship conversation.
- Does the one-sheet with the map on its reverse precede first outreach?
  For a B2B pitch the map-reverse sheet may be the strongest first artefact
  the studio has made — but the ranking's caution stands: brief first,
  quiet build, then the conversation.
