makers themselves, ninety-two named people, and the ritual that makes an
original different from everything else the shop sells.

## The shape of this business

Painted Earth is a **retail business whose product is other people's
work**: what the customer buys is a piece of Irish craft — a card, a
print, a £850 oil — chosen from ninety-two makers under one roundel. The
theatre register should be the table, the work itself — and that is
exactly the lever that is blocked. So the register shifts to what the
block leaves standing: **the maker and the handover**. The names are the
shop. The moment an original changes hands — collection-only, in person,
at 98 Main Street — is the transaction the shop treats differently
already; the record shows it in the caps-locked COLLECTION ONLY warnings
it has to paste into description bodies. The owner test: a small team
with a big dream opens the link and should see their makers' names set
like they matter, and the awkward part of their own shop understood.
Nothing here competes with the live store on imagery — the recorded
reviewer position is accepted as the constraint it is.

## The essence

**A small team with a big dream, and the dream is already true:
ninety-two makers deep, a quarter of the originals sold, and the most
precious stock sold through the same button as a £3 card.**

The paradox is the gap between the shop's care and its tooling: a
business that hand-runs a waiting list inside a sold-out product
description, that warns twice in caps that the kingfisher is collection
only, that keeps 124 sold paintings listed like a roll of honour — all of
it held together by a small team's effort where the platform runs out.
The concept's job is not to out-design a polished store; it is to show
that someone read the shop closely enough to see where the effort goes.

## The diagnosis

1. **The makers are data, not presences.** The snapshot holds all
   ninety-two names; the concept uses them as filter values. A shop that
   is *made of* makers shows none of them — the one recognition move that
   needs no photography at all.
2. **The collection ritual is presented as a warning, not a moment.** The
   concept correctly surfaces collection-only terms before checkout — but
   as logistics. The record says the shop itself feels originals are
   different; the ritual deserves staging, not disclosure alone.
3. **The arc is unused.** Twenty years, the 2025 expansion, the new
   gallery — verified, dated, and absent from the page.
4. **The smallest fixable problem sits waiting.** The hand-run waiting
   list is named in the publication record as worth raising in any
   conversation; nothing on the page shows the studio found it.
5. **The blocked lever is acknowledged but not designed for.** The tiles
   are honestly labelled; but the page has no designed answer to the
   question the owner will ask first — *where does my photography go?* —
   and the answer should be visible, not implicit.

## The moves

Recognition without photography, in build order — and deliberately fewer
than other briefs, because the honest artefact is the conversation.

### 1. Set the makers' roll

The essence made visible: the ninety-two makers, set as a register —
names from the dated snapshot, typeset with the care the roundel
promises, each linked to its real collection on the live shop. No
biographies beyond the makers' own published words; no imagery; the
names themselves are the content, because the names are the shop. The
catalogue snapshot date travels with the roll as data freshness ("as of
26 July 2026") — not as a studio audit trail. The voice work lands beside
it: the gallery's own sentence and one harvest from the three named
visitor reviews, verbatim and attributed lightly, per the Kent and Tool
Centre pattern (month/year on the guest page; full harvest trail in
research).

### 2. Stage the handover

The collection-only ritual given its moment: an original is not posted,
it is *collected* — from 98 Main Street, from the lightfilled upstairs
gallery the shop opened in April 2025. The concept's fulfilment panel
already carries the terms; the elevation is the framing — the romance of
the handover told straight, with the shop's real terms (gift wrap,
dispatch, returns) kept exactly as published. Click-and-collect stays
what it is: a labelled proposal, never an implication.

### 3. Name the waiting list

The smallest move and the sharpest proof of attentiveness: the workshop
loop shown as found — four real workshops, all unavailable, the waiting
list running by hand in a product description — with the concept's
answer kept to a labelled proposal. Only the four real records; no
invented dates, prices or tutors.

### 4. Write the engagement ask into the work

The move the ranking named as the honest next artefact. The concept's
placeholder tiles already say what they are; the elevation is to make the
unblocking visible — the designed home for the shop's own photography,
stated as the one thing the studio cannot supply: *your work, shown as
yours, the moment you license it*. That line is the pitch, and it is
also true of every move above: the makers' roll, the handover, the
waiting list all become stronger the day the shop engages. Outreach
itself remains a separate decision in `PLAN.md`; this brief changes the
page, not the pipeline.

## Recommended bundles

- **First pass (no new assets, no new data):** moves 1 and 2 — the
  makers' roll from the existing snapshot, the handover staging, the
  voice quotes.
- **Second pass:** move 3 — the workshop finding, small and exact.
- **The conversation:** move 4, timed with the outreach decision it
  serves — not before it.

## Honesty constraints

- **Guest voice is the shop's voice.** No studio narration of the elevation
  method, the blocked lever, or the disagreement protocol on the guest
  page. Move 4's engagement ask may name photography as the missing piece
  in plain shop-facing language; it must not read as a research memo.
- **The repair-case line is absolute.** The live shop is polished and the
  page says so; no "before/after", no implication of failure, no
  repetition of the stale 26 July filters claim — the concept names the
  live filters it knows about.
- **No photography until it is licensed.** Every tile stays a visibly
  labelled drawn placeholder; no drawing ever reads as a named maker's
  work; the product record is attributed to the maker and the drawing to
  nobody. The Mourne Cycles deletion precedent governs. Placeholder
  disclosure stays short on the guest layer; fuller boundary text belongs
  in the banner and Sources & limits.
- **Snapshot discipline.** Makers, counts, prices and availability come
  from the dated 26 July 2026 snapshot and are presented as of that
  catalogue date; nothing claims to be the live catalogue. Do not add
  studio re-check dates beside the figures.
- **Maker biographies only in the makers' own published words; reviews
  verbatim, attributed lightly on the guest page** (platform + month/year),
  with full read dates in research. No invented praise, no invented lives.
  Prefer `Visitor review · July 2025` over `shown as "N months ago" · read
  …`.
- **The four workshop records are the whole workshop truth.** No new
  dates, no "regular programme", no availability claims.
- **Click-and-collect is a proposal wherever it appears**, and no
  fulfilment term exceeds the published ones.
- **No relationship implied.** Not contacted, not client work, not a
  pilot; the concept does not move the outreach decision by styling
  itself as already answered.
- Any change to the first viewport recaptures the comparison stills and
  clip in the same commit, per `docs/MEDIA_CAPTURE.md`.

## Tests that pin the elevation

- Assert every maker name on the roll matches the dated snapshot, the
  catalogue date ("as of 26 July 2026" or equivalent) renders with it, and
  every name links to the real live collection — with no studio read stamp
  beside the roll.
- Assert no raster catalogue imagery appears anywhere in the concept, and
  every artwork tile carries a short placeholder label (not an
  over-explained research caveat).
- Assert the shop's quoted sentences and any review harvest are verbatim
  against the recorded source, with light guest attribution (platform +
  month/year; not `· read …` or `shown as`).
- Assert the workshop section contains only the four real records and the
  proposal is labelled.
- Assert the engagement ask names photography as the missing piece without
  implying contact, approval or a pilot, and without studio/meta narration.
- Run the guest-voice guard and the publication checks; then the swap
  test: remove the makers' names, 98 Main Street and the roundel — if the
  page still works for any gift shop, the elevation has not gone far
  enough.

## Open decisions

- Does the makers' roll ship while the concept's imagery is blocked —
  recognition that visibly cannot complete — or does it wait so the shop
  first sees it whole? This brief's position: ship it; the roll needs no
  photography to be true, and it is the strongest argument the
  conversation has.
- The roll names ninety-two third-party makers on a concept page. Names
  plus links to the shop's own collections is plain attribution, but the
  reviewer who recommended stopping this concept may read any expansion
  as doubling down. The disagreement protocol applies: record, don't
  settle silently.
- Does the waiting-list move risk reading as criticism of a shop practice
  the small team may be proud of handling personally? Framing matters:
  the finding is effort, not failure.
- When the engagement conversation happens, is the ask photography first,
  the pilot second — or one conversation? The publication record says the
  imagery swap is the first improvement to raise; the rest is sequencing.
