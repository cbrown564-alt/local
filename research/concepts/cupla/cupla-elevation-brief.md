## The shape of this business

Cúpla is a **counter business**: what the customer buys is a coffee and
something to eat, today, from the counter at 105 Main Street. The
register is **appetite and the day's rhythm** — morning bakes into brunch
bowls, the counter changing as the day does. The concept competes with
the incumbent medium — the Instagram and Facebook accounts where the café
posts today's counter and today's hours — so the feed test governs
everything: concede the day's specifics to the feed on purpose, and win
on what a feed cannot hold. The name explained. The pair. The whole offer
in both languages at once. A menu card worth printing.

## The essence

**Everything here comes in twos: twin owners, two languages, one
counter.**

The name is the Irish for twins; twin owners opened the café in 2024; the
branding pairs Irish and English. The paradox the method asks for is here
in a small, sharp form: a business named after a pair whose pair is
nowhere to be found. The record holds no owner names, no photograph, no
sentence of either twin's — the twins are the whole identity and entirely
anonymous in everything the café has published. That is the finding the
brief builds on: the page can stage the pair honestly — the fact, the
founding, the name — and must design the home for the story only the
twins can tell, without telling it for them.

## The diagnosis

1. **The essence is stated, not staged.** "Twin owners" is one clause
   mid-lede; the name's meaning is a bolded gloss. Nothing on the page
   makes the pair present — the thing the café is named after gets less
   space than the menu disclaimer.
2. **Theatre is missing.** The page is type and SVG chrome from top to
   bottom, and since the restructure it has lost the one warm asset it
   had: the faithful frontage visualisation renders on neither route. The
   café is a real, specific, good-looking shopfront; the page shows none
   of it.
3. **The day's rhythm sits behind struck-through cells.** The rail's
   *Maidin · Morning* and *Lón · Lunch* are inert placeholders — the
   café's actual mechanism rendered as page damage. The 31 July audit is
   blunt: on a concept whose craft thesis is bilingual pairing,
   struck-through Irish reads as error, not honesty.
4. **The swap test half-passes.** Palette, mark and bilingual pairing are
   Cúpla-only — the archived review confirms the identity breaks under
   swap — but the composition is the shared chassis and nothing stages
   *this* café's day. Recognition shipped; theatre missing, exactly as
   the ranking said.
5. **Carried repair debts sit on load-bearing components.** The archived
   review's required repairs and the audit's critical finding touch
   exactly the components the elevation moves will rebuild: a focus ring
   invisible on the petrol surfaces, the phone first viewport showing the
   counter panel above the wordmark, nine Irish strings without
   `lang="ga"`, and the *Babhla Blasta* gloss (*blasta* means tasty;
   savoury would be *goirt*). Fold them into the passes below rather than
   spending a third standalone repair cycle.

## The moves

Ordered by the recommended build sequence: voice first, then the essence
made visible, then theatre, then the keepable artifact.

### 1. Credit the three words

"Brews, Bakes & Bowls" is the café's own and already the page's
structure — *Caife · Bácús · Babhlaí* beneath it. The voice move is to
keep those three words doing that work everywhere: leading the story
column, naming the menu's three columns, naming the day's two beats. No
new studio copy in the café's voice; the discipline the thin record
demands is that these three words, the name and the founding fact carry
the whole page.

### 2. Stage the pair

Replace the inert "Ár scéal · Our story" with a short, honest block built
from the three verified facts: the name is the Irish for twins; twin
owners opened the café in 2024; Est. 2024 is on the door. The pairing
device does the telling — the twin-ring mark, Irish and English running
side by side — so the page *performs* the pair rather than claiming it.
No names, no biography, no invented detail: the record holds none, and
the block says nothing the café has not published. The section is the
designed home for whatever the twins one day want told — and it must
never read as if they have already told it.

### 3. Stage the day in two beats

The theatre, in the Counter register: the counter's own rhythm made
visible. Two beats — **Maidin**, coffee and the morning bakes; **Lón**,
the brunch bowls — replacing the rail's dead cells with the working
structure of the café's day. Each beat carries its part of the offer and
one honest handoff: what's on the counter today, and today's hours, live
on the Instagram the café already runs. The concession is the design, per
the feed test — a page that never goes stale because it never pretends to
know today. No invented hours, no invented specials, no schedule the
record does not hold: the beats are phases of the day, not timetabled
claims.

### 4. Bring the frontage home

Restore the faithful frontage visualisation to the home route — the
concept's subject proof and its one moment of warmth — with the
disclosure chain intact and the phone-fold repairs landed in the same
pass: the wordmark above the fold and the image beginning high enough to
matter at 390px, the fascia lettering inside the crop, the
generated-origin caption hit-test visible above the fixed banner. This is
the moment worth showing to the other twin: their shopfront, recognised.
Before any external use, record in `ATTRIBUTION` the rights position for
the privately held reference photograph the visualisation derives from —
the question the July review left open.

### 5. Make the menu card keepable

The keepable artifact already exists in draft: the bilingual menu card
the archived review called the best-made artefact in the concept — "it
reads as a printed menu, not a web list". What stands between it and the
counter is the named dependency, and it gates this move: **a fluent Irish
speaker reviews the bilingual menu before any client use.** Until then
the card stays labelled a sample. After it, the card is the thing the
owners hold onto — the menu as bilingual as the name, printable and
correct in both languages, with the one-sheet carrying it.

## Honesty constraints

- **No owner names, no biography, no twin-story details.** The record
  holds the fact of twin ownership and nothing else; the pair is staged
  from that fact, the 2024 founding and the door decal — never performed
  with invented names, photographs or anecdotes. Anything the twins
  supply later has a designed home; until they supply it, the home stays
  open rather than filled.
- **Irish is a truth surface.** This concept has already shipped invented
  Irish once (*Gránáit* = granite) and one loose gloss remains. Every
  Irish string is checked, glossed accurately and marked `lang="ga"`; the
  fluent-speaker review is the precondition for client use; and the
  concept never implies the café itself wrote the bilingual treatment —
  the page keeps framing it as drawn from the café's own name and
  branding.
- **"Brews, Bakes & Bowls" is quoted as theirs**, sourced to the café's
  public pages, read 21 July 2026. No other sentence is put in the café's
  mouth.
- **No opening hour, schedule or day's-special claim anywhere.** Hours
  and today's counter live on Instagram; the concept states the handoff,
  never a time.
- **No geography beyond the verified address.** No bay, castle, mountain
  view or village-scene copy; the record holds 105 Main Street and
  nothing more.
- **The disambiguation stands:** Co. Down on every route, distinguishing
  the Utah coffee business and the Dublin suburb.
- **The visualisation keeps its full disclosure chain** — alt text,
  visible caption, case-study Sources & limits, provenance — and the
  rights position for its reference photograph is recorded before any
  external use. Withdrawn imagery stays withdrawn; the frontage is
  depicted only through the documented reference, with nothing invented.
- Any change to the first viewport recaptures the comparison stills and
  clip in the same commit, per `docs/MEDIA_CAPTURE.md`.

## When a move ships

Record the source and read date for every new fact in `research/` and in
the case-study Sources & limits block — that is studio provenance, not guest
copy. Guest-facing attribution stays light (who spoke + a reasonable
month/year or era); never put a studio read stamp, relative "shown as N months
ago", or research caveats on the guest page. Speak as the business. Add
provenance for any new asset in the same commit, update Sources & limits, and
add journey coverage for new behaviour.

Update this status line as moves land; do not call the
concept validated until an owner or representative visitor has supplied
that evidence.
