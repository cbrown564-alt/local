## The organising principle

Facebook already wins at this season on the balcony. Logged out: April 4 2026
Easter post; Jay Wilson / Jacqui and Ritchie, 16 June 2026, naming **Maria**;
reels 565–3.8K views; phone-shot weather and tea on the rail. 829 followers,

The site already holds the brochure the feed cannot: nine themed room names,
£80–£120, breakfast 8–10, ramp and ground-floor access, parking, checkout
11:00, a geography essay, enquiry form. It also holds the problem: 39 gallery
images, almost all `IMG_20190520_*` — one shoot, 20 May 2019. Copy still says
*newly-refurbished*. "Book Now" is an Elementor form whose button is labelled
**Sumbit**. No calendar, no deposit terms, no check-in time, no host name.

So:

- **Anything the feed already does well — this Easter, this balcony, Maria —
  hand off honestly.** Do not rebuild a scrapbook of 2019 JPEGs as if they
  were this morning.
- **Anything a feed cannot hold — which named room, what it costs, whether
  you can get in with a chair, the promenade as the point — is where
  greatness lives.** The concept competes with a four-page OceanWP site that
  already has the words and has frozen the pictures.

## The essence

**Wake on Central Promenade. The purple door is the house; "Book Now" is not
a booking.**

The paradox is infrastructure and time. They have nine rooms with names
(The New Yorker, The Regency, Little Havana, Key West, La Parisienne, Walsh's
Retreat, The Olive, Hollywood, Sorrento) and a public rate card, and the only
action is a contact form with a typo. They say newly refurbished and show a
2019 photoshoot. Facebook says family-run and names Maria; the site never
does. A concept that invents a live calendar they do not have is a different
business. A concept that only lists £80/£100/£120 is a flyer. Recognition
is Enniskeen at B&B scale: *the route into a night at this door*, using their
promenade sentence and their room names, without pretending the gallery is
current photography of this season.
