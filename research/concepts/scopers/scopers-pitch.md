## What this pitch is not

It is not a criticism of their social media. Their Instagram is good: the
food photography is genuinely better than most restaurants in the county
manage, and 5,976 people follow it. The gap is not the posting. It is that
everything they publish is only reachable by people who already follow them,
and expires down a feed within days.

## The opening (say this first, and mean it)

> "Your food posts are the best of any place around here — I'm not here to
> tell you to do social media differently. It's just that everything you put
> up is only findable by the people who already follow you."

## Sixty seconds, standing at the counter

Hello, I'm Conor — I run a small web studio here in the village, Mourne Made.

Before taking up your time I did something. On a phone this morning, logged
out — the way somebody who doesn't follow you sees you — I tried two things.

First, what time you're open today and what's on. Your Facebook page opens on
a cookie box that takes a full screen of scrolling before you can even get rid
of it, and when you do, there are no opening hours on the page at all and no
menu. Just "Closing soon".

Second, the next supper club. On Instagram, logged out on a phone, your
profile shows your name, your bio and nothing else — no posts at all. So the
only way is Facebook: the cookie box again, then Facebook's own "log in to see
more" panel drops over the feed, and the 26th of August is four and a half
screens further down, in a post from the 10th of July. And there's a post from
the 3rd saying the 12th of August, so you have to work out which is newer.

So I built you one — a page where the food is the first thing you see and the
supper club has a permanent home with the date on it. Everything still books
through your Instagram inbox, because that already works. Can I show you? It's
one minute.

*(Show the concept on the phone. Leave the one-sheet. Then:)*

It's independent work — not commissioned, and I'm not presenting it as
something you've approved. If it's useful, the before-and-after is free and
there's no obligation. If you'd rather it came down, I'll take it down that
day.

## What stays (lead with this if they bristle)

- **Their Instagram and Facebook.** Nothing replaces them. The concept links
  to both and hands every booking to the Instagram inbox they already answer.
- **Their words.** Every dish name and description on the page is quoted from
  their own captions.
- **Their badge.** The concept uses their own mark, not a redraw of it.
- **Their menu and their hours** — the page carries no prices and no menu of
  its own; it links out to the Facebook page they already post them on.

## What is *not* theirs, and say so unprompted

The food pictures are AI-generated illustrations, not their photographs. Say
it before they ask, and say why: their own photographs stay off a public site
until they choose otherwise. **If they want their own photography on it, that
is a yes — it makes the page better, and it takes an afternoon.**

## Objections

**"We do fine on Instagram."**
You do — better than fine. This is for the people who don't follow you yet.
Logged out on a phone, your Instagram profile shows no posts at all; this is
the shop window for those people. And it keeps your Instagram inbox as the
booking route, because that already works.

**"We haven't time to keep a website up to date."**
Then don't. The menu button goes to your Facebook, where you already post it.
The only thing worth changing on the page is the supper-club date, once a
month, and I'll show you how in five minutes — or send it to me and I'll do it.

**"Who asked you to do this?"**
Nobody. It's independent and labelled that way everywhere it appears — that's
why I'm standing here telling you rather than putting it out as if you'd hired
me. If you'd rather it came down, I'll take it down.

**"What does it cost?"**
The before-and-after is free. If you want work after that, I quote a fixed
price in writing first, and the first job can be one page.

**"Is that our food in the pictures?"**
No — and I've labelled it on the page. They're illustrations. Your own
photographs are better, and if you want them on it, we use yours.
