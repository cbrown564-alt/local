# Scopers — elevation brief: from good to great, on its own terms

Written 31 July 2026, after the review that found the Scopers concept "good,
not yet great" against `docs/good-to-great-concept-elevation.md` — and after
the pushback that the playbook's seven moves were shaped for a hotel: place,
grounds, rooms, dusk windows. Scopers has none of those raw materials. This
brief re-derives the elevation from the materials this business actually has,
and supersedes the Scopers section of that review.

**Status: thinking only, nothing built.** Every move below needs its own
sourcing pass before a line of code is written; the honesty constraints at the
end decide what each move is allowed to say.

## The organising principle

The concept competes with Scopers' own Instagram, and the feed is genuinely
good at appetite. What a feed cannot hold is permanence, structure and ritual.
So:

- **Anything the feed already does well — today's hours, this week's specials —
  hand off honestly**, exactly as the concept does now. Do not imitate the
  feed; lose to it gracefully and link out.
- **Anything a feed cannot hold — the philosophy, the chef's arc, the larder,
  the invitation — is where greatness lives.** These seven moves all sit on
  that side of the line.

The raw assets, in the order a food bar has them: appetite (perishable — lunch,
today); a philosophy with a mechanism (zero-waste is kitchen logic, not a
vibe); a chef's arc with a paradox in it (Great British Menu 2021, then home
to bake milk bread buns at dawn); a game (the supper club is secret, location
revealed the week before); and a voice (their captions are better copy than
anything the studio will write).

## The moves

Ordered by the recommended build sequence, not by number.

### 1. Their voice, not ours — caption pull-quotes

The Enniskeen pull-quote works because a guest said it better than the studio
could. Scopers' version is stronger: quote *Paul*. Set one caption verbatim,
large, as section furniture — *"Carrots like you've never tasted them before"*
is the leading candidate — with the post date and platform named so the owner
can find his own words elevated to art direction. Cheapest move on the list,
possibly the most persuasive. Sources already read 31 July 2026 and listed in
`research/concepts/scopers/scopers-image-brief.md`.

### 2. Nothing wasted, made visible

"Zero-waste" currently survives as one hyphenated compound in the H1 — the
single most distinctive thing about the business, carried by its smallest
element. Give it a feature: trace one ingredient end-to-end through the week's
cooking, anchored on the verbatim carrot caption (*"the side dish that
explains the whole kitchen: what the Mourne Larder gives, cooked properly,
nothing wasted"*). This is the recognition move for a chef — the estate map
said "we walked your grounds"; this says "we understood your kitchen." Built
only from what their own captions document.

### 3. Appetite as theatre, first screen

For a hotel the hero is the building at dusk; for a food bar the hero is the
plate, close enough to smell. One signature dish art-directed like a menu
cover — macro, the wild garlic mayo mid-drip — with a subtle CSS/SVG steam
wisp (reduced-motion safe) as the cheap surprise. The current hero leads with
typography and keeps food below the fold; that is hotel thinking. The
day-part move translates better here than anywhere: breakfast in the morning,
the burger at midday, the supper-club table in the evening — appetite states,
not just light.

**Guardrail:** the swap must never imply open or closed. Hours are irregular
and live on Facebook; the day-part changes appetite, never availability. The
"Today's hours on Facebook" strip stays untouched.

**Creative fork to decide before generating:** photoreal vs illustration.
Photoreal imitates the feed and carries a standing honesty tension — it looks
like a photograph of their food until the caption is read. A commissioned
cookbook/menu-cover illustration style is more honest, differentiates from
the feed instead of competing with it, and is more ownable across the
one-sheet and print. Photoreal is more appetizing. This is a deliberate
choice, not a default; the existing six images are photoreal, so switching is
a regeneration, not a restyle.

### 4. The counter

The format is the identity: no table service, no lunch bookings, you queue
and point. Art-direct the food section *as the counter* — dishes in a row
like the glass display, named like chalkboard labels — with the honest
scarcity line as furniture: *"Baked this morning. When it's gone, it's gone."*
(grounded in the bread caption, "every bun, every loaf", baked each day).
Scarcity is appetizing, true, and something no templated restaurant site ever
says, because templates assume bookings.

### 5. The invitation

The supper club stops being a details card and becomes the game it actually
is. The page as a sealed invitation: the round badge already reads like a wax
seal, so the motif is theirs, not invented; the date as the hero; "the
location is revealed to guests the week before" dramatised rather than
footnoted; past events as clues rather than a list — **documented events
only**. The theatre is already paid for inside the business; the concept just
stages it. The next-date line keeps its re-verification rule from
`research/concepts/scopers/scopers-pitch.md` — on this business the date is the pitch.

### 6. The menu is a map

The estate-map analog, but it matters more here: for the hotel the grounds
are an amenity; for Scopers the ten square miles around Dundrum are the
supply chain and the creed. A drawn map of the Mourne Larder — the bay, the
dunes, the hedgerows, the foothills — in the concept's palette, each signature
dish pinned to its place. **Indicative disclosure as with the estate map, and
drawn only from what Scopers publicly says about its sourcing: no named
farms, no invented suppliers.** This is the keepable artifact and the session
sized item — budget it alone, per the estate-map lesson in the playbook.

### 7. From the banquet to the bar

The chef's arc as a short, quiet timeline: GBM 2021 → coming home → the bar
opens (July 2022, from the fourth-birthday post of 23 July 2026) → four years
of the counter → the supper clubs. The paradox is the headline — the man who
cooked for the nation now judges his day in milk bread buns. Dates only where
documented, per the Enniskeen timeline rule.

## Recommended bundles

- **Cheap bundle (hours):** moves 1, 2, and the first-screen half of 3 (one
  hero dish; the existing generations already provide the asset if photoreal
  stays). All recognition, no restructuring.
- **Theatre bundle:** 5 and the day-part half of 3.
- **Artifact session:** 6, alone, then the one-sheet — which for a food
  business should be designed as a menu card, and takes the map on its
  reverse exactly as the Enniskeen one-sheet takes the estate map.

## Honesty constraints (decide everything above)

- The privacy decision of 31 July 2026 stands: public site AI-imagery only;
  their own photographs for personal outreach material only.
- Dish names, quotes and dates come from their own public posts, read and
  recorded the same way as `research/pipeline/verifications.json` and
  `research/image-provenance.md`. Nothing invented for
  atmosphere; no named suppliers, no fabricated past supper-club locations,
  no opening-hours claims.
- Every generated image keeps the triple disclosure established by the image
  brief: alt text, a visible line on the page, and the case study's Sources &
  limits block. If the illustration fork in move 3 is taken, the disclosure
  wording changes from "not photographs" to match — and gets *easier* to keep
  honest, which is half the point.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` and the existing studio-banner conventions apply
  to the steam and any day-part behaviour, per standing studio rules.
- None of the moves touch the booking handoff (Instagram DM) or the
  hours handoff (Facebook). Those are real and they work.

## When a move ships

Each landed move gets: provenance entries for any new file in the same
commit; a check in the concept journey coverage (and the case for a
`test:scopers` suite grows with each one — the date, the provenance line and
first-screen ordering are currently pinned by nothing); and a note in this
file's status line, as the Enniskeen briefs do.
