## The insight the first pass missed

Read the feed as a whole, not as a list of facts, and something strange
surfaces: **this guesthouse never shows you a room.** Not one bedroom, not
one tariff, not one "book now", not one availability post — on the only live
surface the house has. The grid is dogs on the wood floor and on the lawn,
cyclists loading panniers, motorbikes at the door, the fry, the garden, the
Castlewellan Show flyer, the castle heritage day, Cash for Kids. The one
readable caption of 2026 is addressed to people who have *already stayed*:
*"What wonderful people we had last night 🙂 Off today to do 'Pippen's
Coastal run 2026'"*.

The house's public voice is a **guestbook**, not a brochure. It is written in
the past tense of hospitality — last night's people, this morning's dog run —
for an audience of friends and returning guests. The only brochure it ever
had was the website: rooms, tariffs, breakfast times. That site is now a
parked domain, and the house never missed it, because its trade never came
from strangers on the web. It comes from the phone, from word of mouth, from
cyclists who come back. Twelve Facebook reviews, 100% recommend, and not one
night for sale anywhere on the live web.

That is the paradox, and it is documented, not invented: **a business whose
entire trade is nights, whose public record contains not one night for sale —
and whose diary never noticed.**

The concept's job follows from it. A templated B&B site would invent the
thing the house itself has never published — a room grid, an amenities list,
a booking widget — and would be *less* like the house than the house's own
Facebook page. The elevated concept is the opposite: **the arrival the feed
never was.** A front door for the stranger, written in the guestbook's own
voice, selling exactly what the house sells — the wake-up, the village, the
welcome — and routing the one transaction the house has (the night) to the
one place it has always lived (the phone). The thin record is not the
weakness of this concept. Owned honestly, it *is* the concept.

## The organising principle

Facebook already wins at proof of welcome, and it wins in the house's own
grammar. What a feed cannot hold is permanence, geography and arrival: where
you wake, how a stranger gets a night, the village as the point. So:

- **Anything the feed already does well — last night's guests, the dog, the
  next village day, the fry — hand off honestly.** The concept quotes one
  anchored line and sends the reader to the feed for the rest. It does not
  rebuild a scrapbook the studio cannot keep current.
- **Anything a feed cannot hold — the door, the map, the way in — is where
  greatness lives.** The dead site is not a competitor. It is the gap the
  concept fills, and it is never sneered at on the guest page (Buck's Head
  rule).

## The essence

**Wake in Dundrum. The house shows you everything except the rooms — the
rooms are a phone call.**

The first screen keeps "Wake in Dundrum. Newcastle when you want it." as the
lede; the essence section below it says the quieter, truer thing in the
house's own voice. The swap test bites here: remove the guestbook register
and the page is any coastal B&B; with it, the page could not be anyone else's,
because no other business's record *has* this shape.

**Register correction.** The shipped hero is Enniskeen's move transplanted:
dusk windows, arrival, atmosphere. Arley's own grammar is the *morning after*
— the fry, the dog's run, the cyclists leaving. "Wake in Dundrum" is a
morning promise wearing a dusk plate. Move 4 specs the morning hero; the
mechanism ships before the asset (playbook lesson).

## The diagnosis

Against this harvest and the shipped `src/concepts/arley-house/home.astro`:

1. **The page is studio-voiced between two of their sentences.** Their Intro
   sits at the top as decoration and their 25 July line sits at the bottom as
   a footnote; everything between is us. The guestbook register — the thing
   that makes this house unlike any other record in the portfolio — never
   takes over the page.
2. **The essence is unsaid.** The one thing no template would ever claim —
   this house has never sold a night online and has never needed to — is the
   most persuasive thing on the page, and the page does not say it. The thin
   record is being hidden rather than owned.
3. **"Find your way around" cards fail the swap test.** Three title/note
   cards ("The village", "Dundrum Castle", "Beaches either side") are any
   coastal stay anywhere. The Intro sentence is a geography claim; geography
   deserves the drawn artifact, not cards.
4. **The hero is the wrong moment of the day.** Dusk windows is the hotel
   register. This house's promise is the morning.
5. **No photographic voice.** One plate, no grade; any second asset would
   read as a different shoot.
6. **The dead site is the unmined argument** — for the case study and the
   pitch, not the guest page.

## The moves

Ordered by build sequence: voice first, then the essence made visible, then
the artifact, then theatre. Guest copy below is verbatim intent — the build
should ship these words, adjusting only for fit.

### 1. Their voice, not ours — the Intro, untidied *(shipped; keep)*

The Intro stays as hero furniture with its punctuation intact, Facebook
findable. What changes is that it stops being the *only* moment of their
voice: the whole page now speaks guestbook.

### 2. The essence made visible: "Everything but the rooms"

New section, in the house's voice, owning the paradox warmly — the
recognition move. Verbatim guest copy:

> **Everything but the rooms.**
>
> The dog on the boards. Bikes at the door. The fry in the morning and the
> village days as they come — that is all on our page, and always will be.
> The one thing we have never put on it is a bedroom.
>
> The rooms are kept the way they have always been kept: ring the house,
> tell us the nights you have in mind, and we will tell you what we have.

No room grid, no amenities list, no invented inventory — because the house
itself has never published one on a live page. This is the anti-generic move:
it converts the thin record into the concept's spine, and it makes the phone
feel like the house's choice rather than its limitation. The "The house /
Family-run, in the village" section folds into this.

### 3. The map is the promise — the keepable artifact *(this pass)*

The Intro sentence is a geography claim — *"perfect to explore this Area of
Outstanding Natural Beauty"* — and geography is the one thing we can draw
honestly. A hand-drawn SVG plate in the concept palette (MapPlate, the
kelly-mcevoy-brown pattern — no generation session needed), replacing the
"Find your way around" cards:

- **Belfast Road as the spine** — the road the house is addressed on, drawn
  as a paired penned line from the village toward the top of the plate,
  because it is how guests arrive. (The archive calls it the A24; that is
  archival voice and the number is not clearly right for Dundrum — the plate
  names the street, not a road number.)
- **Pins, all sourced to Layer A or the attributed Visit Mourne sentence:**
  Arley House, 14 Belfast Road (the only pin with a halo — the house is the
  point); Dundrum village; Dundrum Castle ("a short walk" — live 2026
  heritage flyer plus the Visit Mourne sentence); the bay with beaches either
  side of the village (the Visit Mourne sentence); Newcastle down the road.
- **The Mournes** as layered ridge marks along the south, because the Visit
  Mourne sentence ends "where the Mountains of Mourne sweep down to the sea".
- A compass; indicative caption in the house's voice.

Caption: *"A sketch of the village and the bay as we describe them to guests —
indicative, not a survey."* Section lead: *"Wake on Belfast Road. The castle
on its hill, the bay below the village, beaches either side — and Newcastle
down the road when you want it."* No driving times (the "five minute trip" is
archival voice; the map shows the proximity instead of claiming it). The map
is the one-sheet reverse when the sheet is printed.

### 4. Theatre: the morning plate *(mechanism now, asset on generation)*

Spec for the generation session, in the register the house trades in:
**the door in early light** — low sun down Belfast Road, bikes leaning by the
door, a big white dog on the boards, the day's first fry implied rather than
shown. Photoreal, triple-disclosed, composed from what the grid actually
contains (door, bikes, dog — all Layer A). When it lands it takes the hero;
the dusk plate moves down-page as the evening beat of the same day, or is
retired. The hero's composition, veil and disclosure ship now so the swap is
one asset (playbook: ship the mechanism before the assets).

### 5. The ritual staged: the diary is the phone *(shipped; sharpen)*

The call-script form stays — it is the correct honest mechanism and the
"ask about a dog" chip is the kennels honesty in one control. Sharpen the
lead to name the second sheet: *"Write the nights. Ring the house — or send
the same words by email. These words are for the call; nothing is sent from
here."* No booking engine, no 2017 prices, no "Always open".

### 6. One photographic voice — the house grade

A shared CSS grade over every plate (custom property, composable, the
Enniskeen `--enk-grade` lesson: it must chain, not override), so current and
future generated imagery reads as one commissioned morning: warm highlights,
slightly eased saturation, a soft lift in the shadows. Cheap; it is what
separates art direction from assembled generations.

### 7. The guestbook band

The 25 July caption moves up from a footer strip into a proper band, anchored
as the page's emotional close, with the honest feed handoff in the house's
voice: *"The days as they happen — the dog, the runs, the shows — are on our
Facebook."* No resurrected 2012 testimonials, no invented review text. One
anchored line, dated lightly (July 2026), then the door out to the feed.
