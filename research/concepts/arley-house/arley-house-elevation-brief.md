## The organising principle

Facebook already wins at proof of welcome. Logged out on 23 August 2026 the
page shows dogs on wood floors and on the lawn, cyclists with panniers, two-up
motorcycles at the door, an ulster fry, garden flowers, Castlewellan Show and
Dundrum Castle heritage flyers, Cash for Kids, and reels that travel (view
counts up to 2.8K). The one readable caption is dated 25 July 2026: *"What
wonderful people we had last night 🙂 Off today to do 'Pippen's Coastal run
2026'"*. Intro: *"Arley House.. a family run B&B , is perfect to explore this
Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty"*. 612 followers, 12 reviews at 100%
recommend, landline and email in About. The register is animal-and-guest-led,
not room-led.

The house's own website, still listed on Facebook, is **dead**. On 23 August
2026 `arleyhousedundrum.co.uk` is a registrar parking frameset. There is no
live brochure, no tariff, no booking path. Everything room-shaped we have is
nine years old and must stay archival.

So:

- **Anything the feed already does well — last night's guests, the dog, the
  next village day, the fry — hand off honestly.** Do not rebuild a guest
  scrapbook the studio cannot keep current.
- **Anything a feed cannot hold — where you wake, how you get a night, the
  village as the point rather than Newcastle-as-address — is where greatness
  lives.** The dead site is not a competitor. It is the gap.

The raw assets: a village address on the Belfast–Newcastle road; a family-run
sentence in their punctuation; a dog-and-bike proof stream; one named animal
in 2026 (Pippen) and one in the archive (Duke); a phone; an email Facebook
lists that the earlier brief said we did not have; and an archival room list
that must not be sold as current.

## The essence

**Wake in Dundrum. Newcastle when you want it. Ring the house — the website is
gone.**

The paradox is no longer only geography. It is also infrastructure: the house
still trades (a July 2026 post, a live phone, dogs and cyclists this season)
and the domain that used to hold rooms and prices now parks. A concept that
sells "Newcastle B&B" on the first line fails the house. A concept that
invents 2026 tariffs from 2017 archive fails the record. A concept that only
lists amenities is any coastal stay. The recognition move is Enniskeen's, at
B&B scale: *the route into a night at this house, without pretending the old
site is still there.*

   diary is the phone). Mailto is now an honest second, if the form still

4. **The Visit Mourne quote is still the strongest place sentence we can
   print without pretending the 2017 site is live.** Keep the attribution.
5. **Amenities on the current page (games room, ground-floor bedrooms, cot,
   parking, wifi, laundry, tea in rooms) are not on live Facebook.** Some
   echo the archive (cots, laundry, wifi, tea, parking). Games room and
   ground-floor bedrooms were not in this harvest. **Unship or source them.**
   Do not leave unverified hotel-spec on a thin record.
6. **Hours: "Always open" is a trap.** Keep "ring the house for nights."
7. **Theatre: dusk windows we have not photographed** has the same honesty
   tension as Scopers' photoreal burger. The feed's real hero is the door

8. **The dead site is the unmined argument.** Enniskeen elevated a 2012

Live-safe: family-run B&B; 14 Belfast Road, Dundrum; ring 028 4375 0949;

wifi, laundry may return only if a 2026 source republishes them, or as a
phone question ("ask about the room"), not as a spec list.
